## 0. The findings that decide the design (read this first)

1. **A POS bill is a GST Sales voucher, and every statutory engine already reads one.** B2C small supplies aggregate by place of supply, intra/inter and rate (`gstr1.ts:546-559`, key `pos|INTRA|rate`; on 500,000 synthetic bills the whole year folds into 4 B2CS rows [M]), the HSN summary has a B2C half (`gstr1.ts:601`, `:1041-1043`), Table 13 groups documents by voucher type and numbering window (`gstr1.ts:424-477`), credit notes net into the same B2CS rows with sign -1 (`gstr1.ts:573-577`), a cancelled bill keeps its number as a shell (`voucherCancel.ts:180-214`), stock and cost come from the log (`inventory.ts:82-173`), and a UPI link and a WhatsApp link exist (`upi.ts:112-130`, `share.ts:127-131`). No parallel sales ledger is needed. [C][M]
2. **The Sales voucher SCREEN is the wrong front end, and the repo already shows the right pattern.** A canonical 10-line GST invoice is 115 key actions, 117 presses, and four prompts (party, place of supply, order reference, sales ledger) come before the first item (`docs/PERF.md:270-298`, `VoucherScreen.tsx:2009-2012`, `:3182-3210`); the tax rows are chosen and typed by hand (`VoucherScreen.tsx:2033-2038`, `:2100-2110`); a Sales voucher refuses to save without a party and a sales ledger (`VoucherScreen.tsx:2400-2405`). A POS needs its **own writer** that builds the same `Voucher`. Bulk Entry is the precedent (tax rows, round-off, all-or-nothing transaction: `BulkEntry.tsx:496-578`, `:943-946`), and the custom-fields branch has already pulled its planner out into a pure file, `core/bulkPlan.ts` (720 lines: a tax ledger found by head and rate, a Round Off ledger found by name, every voucher-screen guard run per row, `BuildCtx` injected so a suite needs no browser) [CF `bulkPlan.ts:189-221`, `:296-302`]: after that branch merges, the POS writer should be written beside it and share what it can, not copy it a third time. [C][CF]
3. **Nothing in the data model is per terminal, per cashier, per shift or per tender.** `Voucher` has a date but no time, no terminal, no operator (only the change-log row carries one), no tender list (`types.ts:991-1049`; `OutboxRecord.operator`, `db.ts:43-69`). And the save is a from-scratch object literal (`VoucherScreen.tsx:2737`), so any new voucher field not added there is erased on every alteration (`docs/research/custom-fields-code-audit.md` finding 4 [R]; I re-read the literal: it names each field one by one). The custom-fields branch answers this for its own fields with a **carry funnel** and a classification suite, `voucher-resave`, that fails on any `Voucher` key nobody classified [CF `core/voucherFields.ts`; CF notes section 2, CF-2]: a POS key has to join that funnel, and the server's keep list (section 12). [C][CF]
4. **Split tenders cannot be added as extra debit legs without corrupting GST.** `voucherGstSummary` adds any non-tax, non-party, non-Round-Off entry to **taxable value** (`gst.ts:161-177`), so a Sales voucher with party = Cash plus a second debit leg on a UPI ledger counts the UPI amount as taxable turnover in GSTR-1, the HSN summary and B2CS. The party contract is read in 23 call sites of `voucherGstSummary` (20 files), 9 of `voucherHeadlineAmount`, 3 of `revenueEntries`, and 267 mentions of `partyId` in 71 files [C Appendix B, re-run]. **Two shapes need no engine change:** a single-tender bill whose party IS the tender ledger (the party picker already offers cash and bank groups, `VoucherScreen.tsx:1965-1971`), and a mixed-tender bill saved with same-transaction Receipt vouchers (section 2.3). A fifth shape, a per-terminal clearing ledger settled once per shift, also needs no engine change and is compared there. [C]
5. **Volume breaks the store long before it breaks IndexedDB, and two engines grow faster than the book.** One terminal makes 109,500, 292,000 or 1,095,000 bills a year at 300, 800 or 3,000 a day. Per bill the voucher screen does work proportional to the whole book: the save copies, filters and re-sorts the voucher array (`store.ts:698-702`); two memos rescan the book (`VoucherScreen.tsx:1367-1373`); and **`nextVchNo` runs on every render of the voucher screen**, which is every keystroke (`VoucherScreen.tsx:1115` calls `store.ts:722-736`). **Re-measured on `HEAD` [M]: 61 ms per call at 120,000 bills, 160 ms at 250,000 and about 400 ms at 500,000 for a type with yearly restart** (8, 28 and 55 ms without restart). **`stockPositions` is not linear on this shape: 82 ms, 257 ms and 1.4 s (1.1 to 2.7 s) at 120,000, 250,000 and 500,000 bills**, and a whole-year `buildGstr1` is 0.8 s, 2.8 s and 13 s (one month of it: 0.9 s at 250,000, 2.4 s at 500,000). Summed from the separately measured parts, one Accept is about 0.5 s at 250,000 bills and about 2.3 s at 500,000, before React renders anything [M, sum, J]. `docs/PERF.md` puts the whole Accept at 223 ms p50 at 250,000 trader vouchers (`PERF.md:178`, `:225`). 250,000 bills is 83 days at 3,000 a day and about a year at 800 a day. [C][M]
6. **Sync cannot carry a multi-terminal store as built.** Every pulled page of 500 rows is followed by `reloadActive()`, which re-reads the whole company from IndexedDB and re-sorts it (`sync.ts:713`; `load.sort` is 201 ms p50 at 250,000 vouchers, `PERF.md:172`). A nudge from another device reaches a terminal within 0.5 to 4 seconds while others are writing (`realtimeCore.ts:30-31`), and a new terminal joining a 250,000-voucher company pulls 502 pages, i.e. 502 full reloads (`mobile-backend-readiness.md` section 5.2 [R]). The outbox is never pruned (`sync.ts:14-22`) and two screens load all of it into memory (`AuditLog.tsx:101`, `cancelled.tsx:48`). [C][R]
7. **Backups stop working at about 290,000 to 370,000 bills.** The auto backup is one pretty-printed JSON string of the whole company (`autoBackup.ts:207`) and the desktop bridge refuses a file over 512 MiB (`main.cjs:35`, `:89`). A 5-line bill is 1,835 bytes pretty-printed and a 4-line bill 1,451 [M], so the cap is hit between **292,572 and about 370,000 bills**: roughly 8 to 10 months at 1,200 a day. Restore has never been tested (`HANDOFF.md:96`). Storage persistence is requested (`data/persist.ts:30-35`), which guards against eviction but not against size. [C][M]
8. **Rule 46 numbering is solvable by series per terminal, and five things in the code would break it.** The server counter is per (tenant, company, voucher type, window) (`numbering.js:7-10`), so one voucher type per terminal is one series per terminal and a consecutive series with no clashes [J]. What breaks it: **Undo** of a just-saved voucher deletes it and leaves a hole (`undo.ts:118-119`); an **Optional** voucher burns a number (`store.ts:734-735` counts every voucher of the type) yet is left out of Table 13 (`gstr1.ts:427`), so it cannot serve as a held bill; **the working date defaults to the last voucher's date, not today** (`store.ts:357`, `:702`, `VoucherScreen.tsx:1111`) and F2 changes it; a signed-in, online Accept waits up to 4 s for a server number (`vchNumbering.ts:20`), pure overhead for a series no other device writes; and **a type set to restart Monthly with no `{mm}` token in its prefix repeats numbers inside one financial year, which Rule 46(b) forbids**, with no warning anywhere (section 4). [C][W]
9. **Pricing is not retail pricing, and the scan engines are not wired.** There is no barcode field in the app (alias and part number are the only match, `VoucherScreen.tsx:2862-2870`), no MRP or tax-inclusive price (`voucherConfig.ts:16-17` says MRP is not built; a rate is held to 2 decimals, `money.ts:132-136`), no bill-level discount, and a line discount exists only when F11 "Use discount column" is on (`VoucherScreen.tsx:1362`, `CompanyCreate.tsx:349`). An unknown barcode typed into the item field fails silently (`VoucherScreen.tsx:2047-2049` returns with no message), and a free item cannot be entered because Enter on a zero amount is refused (`VoucherScreen.tsx:3311-3314`). **What changed since revision 1:** the detector (`core/scanInput.ts`), the exact-lookup resolver (`scanResolve.ts`), weighing-scale label profiles (`scale.ts`), the GS1 and EAN encoders (`core/barcode/*`), the MRP ceiling and first-expiry proposal (`batches.ts`) and the cell and unit engines are merged and tested, **and nothing in `src/` imports any of them** (grep, Appendix B). Until the tracking spec's wiring slice T-5b runs, a counter still scans into a plain text box. [C][S]
10. **Rights are client-side and too coarse for a counter.** A role has areas, voucher bases and the actions create, alter, delete (`types.ts:683-695`, `:681`); there is no cancel, discount, price-override, refund, reprint or no-sale right, a cashier needs the Accounting area which also opens the Day Book and cash book (`access.ts:129-133`), and the server accepts any table name of the right shape and any payload from any member of the tenant (`sync.js:188-192`; `mobile-backend-readiness.md:66` [R]). `access.ts:12-14` states the limit itself. Theft prevention here can only be **detective** until the server enforces something. [C][R]
11. **The hardware path is a browser print dialog.** Every receipt goes through `window.print()` (`reportTools.ts:275`, `PrintVoucher.tsx:608`) on A4, A5 or Letter (`types.ts:186`); the Electron bridge exposes only backup, save-as-PDF and open-a-WhatsApp-link (`preload.cjs:6-43`); there is no silent print, printer list, cash drawer, kiosk mode or single-instance lock (grep, Appendix B). The tracking spec designs a silent, listed-printer print path and a raw bridge, **for TSPL and ZPL label printers, not for ESC/POS receipt printers** [S 13.6], and says plainly that the one-day printed spike that would prove even the browser path has not been run. The live site is plain HTTP, which blocks the camera, service workers and installing as an app (`HANDOFF.md:90`; `mobile-personas-and-jobs.md:108` [R]). [C][S][R]
12. **Customers are ledgers, and day close does not exist.** The party picker matches name and alias only (`VoucherScreen.tsx:1958-1963`), a new customer is the 15-field ledger form (`masters.tsx:354-459`), and 100,000 ledgers make the Day Book's per-row `find` ruinous (`PERF.md:248-253`). There is no shift, float, over/short ledger, Z report or loyalty field anywhere in `src` (Appendix B). [C]
13. **The decision that dominates is voucher-per-bill versus summary posting** (section 3.4). My position [J]: **voucher-per-bill is the system of record**, behind a hot-path rebuild and one company per financial year; summary posting is a later, opt-in close for very high volume and for sub-Rs-200 walk-in sales only. The law sets the floor: a supplier may skip an invoice below Rs 200 only for an unregistered buyer who does not ask for one, and must then issue one consolidated invoice at the close of the day (s.31(3)(b) and the Rule 46 proviso [W]); every other bill needs its own number. ERPNext made the opposite choice (POS invoices are intermediate records and one consolidated sales invoice posts at closing) for exactly the ledger-volume reason [W]; section 3.4 says what that costs here. The numbers that would change my position are bills per day per outlet, the B2B share and the credit share (section 15).
14. **The shared tree is crowded, and the order of the builds is now known.** `VoucherScreen.tsx`, `types.ts`, `store.ts`, `inventory.ts`, `db.ts`, `keys.ts`, `sync.ts` and `backend/src/sync.js` are one-writer files for three other builds: the custom-fields branch (355 files changed against `main`; `VoucherScreen.tsx` alone +677 lines) [CF], the tracking lane (whose own order on the voucher screen is Back's overlay edits, CF-2, CF-3, T-2a, CF-9c, T-2b [S 15.1, 15.3]) and the Back control. A POS should live in new files and touch the shared ones only through the single integration lane in section 14, **after** or **with** those, never in parallel. [C][CF][S]
15. **A POS should be built on the tracking, scan and label designs, not beside them.** Barcode and GS1 reading, pack and variant codes, weighing-scale labels, the first-expiry proposal, the MRP ceiling, expired and held-batch rules, the unit chain and the server's duplicate-IMEI detection, the stock cell `(godown, batch, variant)`, and the label print path (a receipt path would reuse its driver-print leg) are all designed in the tracking spec and mostly written as pure engines; the POS table in section 11 marks which of its needs each one meets and which none does (MRP-first pricing, tenders, shifts, holds, restaurant tables, receipts on ESC/POS, loyalty). A POS that re-implements any of them forks the stock key and the scan layer a third time. [S][C]
16. **The statutory reads that change the code.** (a) A bill of an unregistered buyer under Rs 50,000 carries the buyer's name and address only on request; at Rs 50,000 or more it must (Rule 46(e) and (f) [W]); nothing in the app asks. (b) A QR on a B2C invoice is a legal duty only for a supplier above Rs 500 crore aggregate turnover (Notification 14/2020, Circular 156/12/2021 [W]); for the target shops a UPI QR is a payment convenience and the dynamic QR that carries invoice details is not required. (c) Nobody in the card payment chain except issuers and networks may store actual card data (RBI/2022-23/77 [W]): the POS stores an approval reference and at most the last four digits, never a number. (d) A phone number captured for loyalty or a WhatsApp bill is personal data: the DPDP Rules, notified in November 2025, bring notice and consent duties after an 18-month phase [W PIB; the May 2027 date is [Ws]]. (e) Restaurant service through an aggregator is taxed in the operator's hands from 1 Jan 2022 and has **no code at all** here (section 1.5). (f) A discount recorded on the invoice is left out of the value of supply; one given afterwards needs an agreement made at or before the supply and the recipient's reversal of credit (s.15(3) [W]): so a POS must allocate a bill discount before tax and must send "discount after the sale" through a credit note. (g) From 22 Sep 2025 the rate on apparel and footwear depends on the unit price (5% up to Rs 2,500 a piece, 18% above) and the rate engine has no value rule [R pos-retail-market.md 6; C grep]. [W][R][C]

### 1.2 Speed of entry

- The canonical invoice is 115 actions; finishing by Enter-through is 119; a repeat customer by ghost recall is 7 actions but that 7 skips the bill-wise panel (`PERF.md:270-298`). Per line, nine key presses: three characters and Enter for the item, two digits and Enter for quantity, then Enter on the pre-filled rate and Enter on the computed amount (`PERF.md:276-286`). [C]
- Before the first item: party, place of supply (when GST is on), reference, sales ledger (`VoucherScreen.tsx:2009-2012` `advanceFromParty`, `:3182-3210`). Alt+K can hold party, godown and place of supply across vouchers, session-only (`VoucherScreen.tsx:163-166`, `:1697-1710`). [C]
- Ctrl+A accepts from any field with no prompt (`VoucherScreen.tsx:3007`, comment at `:2335`) but skips the bill-wise panel, so a credit sale saved that way carries no New Ref and is read as On Account by the bill-wise engine (`billwise.ts:51-53`: an entry with no `bills` is On Account). `PERF.md:300-305` flagged this as unverified for the voucher owner; the engine side I did verify. [C]
- **What a POS needs:** a scan-to-bill of one item in about 3 to 5 actions (scan, tender key, optional print), with party, sales ledger, godown, voucher type, tax ledgers and place of supply all defaulted from a terminal profile. None of those defaults exists as a setting today; Tally's equivalent is the POS voucher class (section 2.2) and this app's parity audit lists "voucher classes" as **missing** (`docs/research/tally-parity-recheck-2026-10.md:110` [R]). [J]
- At Accept the screen also checks the number against the IRP's own rules (`einvoiceDoc.ts` `docNoProblem` and `docNoClash`, `VoucherScreen.tsx:2441-2449`): case-insensitive, unique for a financial year and document type, only for an invoice to a party with a GSTIN in a company whose e-invoicing date has passed, and only as a caution. The same shape of check does not exist for a POS series. [C]

### 1.3 Items, pickers, barcodes, price levels

- The item picker is a substring match on name, alias or part number, sorted by name, showing on-hand quantity per row (`VoucherScreen.tsx:2862-2880`). With an empty query it lists every item and the popup renders one DOM row per match with no virtualisation (`PERF.md:331-337`). [C]
- **Barcode in the wired app: none.** No field, no EAN or GTIN type, no scan handler (grep, Appendix B). A keyboard-wedge scanner works only if the code was typed into the item's **alias** (`types.ts:492`) or **part number** (`types.ts:493`): so one barcode per item, no pack-size barcodes, no batch barcodes. The scanner's characters arrive as ordinary key presses (`keys.ts:15-35`), each re-rendering the whole voucher component, including the `nextVchNo` scan of finding 5: **13 characters x 160 ms is about 2.1 s of main-thread work at 250,000 bills for that one call**, on a scanner that sends a character every 5 to 20 ms [M, arithmetic; J on the scanner]. Tally's POS help handles scanning the same way: the 16-digit code goes in the item's Alias and a scan into the item field fills item, rate and quantity; the same page says barcode labels cannot be printed from TallyPrime (re-read 2026-10-25) [W help.tallysolutions.com POS page]. [C][M][W]
- **Unknown barcode fails silently:** Enter on an item name with no match calls `pop?.pick(popSel)` with an empty list, and `pickItem` returns on `!it` with no message (`VoucherScreen.tsx:3211-3227`, `:2047-2049`); the next scan appends to the same text. [C]
- **What now exists beside it, merged and not wired.** `core/scanInput.ts` (a pure state machine that tells a scanner's burst from a person typing, clocked by `KeyboardEvent.timeStamp` so a slow render cannot make a scan look like typing, with rules for Caps Lock, AltGr, input methods, the Alt-keypad mode and two timing-independent backstops on the Accept bar and on Narration's Enter), `scanDevice.ts` (the scanner as a per-device, per-connection fact, with the setup-scan checks that one recorded scan can decide as pure analysis), `scanResolve.ts` (one exact lookup in a fixed order: a unit, a batch code, an item, pack or variant code, a scale code, a GS1 element string, then alias or part number; never a substring filter; an unknown code is said, never guessed), `scale.ts` (profiles for in-store weighing labels with test vectors, among them three letter-prefixed layouts read from GoFrugal's pages), `core/barcode/*` (EAN, UPC, Code 128, Code 39, ITF, GS1 strings, QR symbol) and `batches.ts` (`proposeAllocation`, the first-expiry-first-out pick; `mrpCeiling`) [C file headers; S 14.2 to 14.4, 4.4]. **A grep for an import of any of them from outside the engine files finds nothing** (Appendix B). The wiring (slice T-5b: one capture-phase listener in `keys.ts`, an uncontrolled `ScanInput` that tells the screen once per run, the item field as a scan target, "Create item with this code") is not built and waits for Back's edit of `keys.ts` [S 14.3, 15.1]. The spec's own scan budget (a 13-character scan at 3 ms spacing in at most 1.5 s at 120,000 vouchers and a 30,000-item master) "is only reachable if a scan is at most two state changes", and its text quotes this audit's per-render figure [S 3.7, 14.2]: **lane A of section 14 (the hot path) is therefore a precondition of the scan layer's budget, not only of the POS.** [C][S]
- **What the POS must do with them [J].** Register its own item field as a scan target on the same stack the spec defines (`registerScanTarget`, [S 14.3]) and never read keys or timing itself; take a resolved result (item, pack with `packQty`, variant, batch, unit, weighed quantity) and not text; and offer "create item with this code" only to an operator holding the Masters right. A scan of a unit (an IMEI) on a POS bill is the spec's outward flow: the unit is judged against the unit chain at once and a repeat says "already on line 3" and never increments [S 14.5].
- **Price levels (wave 3):** the order is the level picked on the voucher (Alt+Z), then the party's default level, then none; an item takes the level's listed rate, else its standard selling rate (`priceLevel.ts:18-41`, wired at `VoucherScreen.tsx:2063-2066` and `:1483-1488`). This fits Retail, Wholesale and Staff lists. A listed rate of 0 shows as blank, "no rate for this level" (`priceLevel.ts:33-34`). [C]
- **MRP-first pricing is designed nowhere; only an MRP check is.** Indian packaged goods carry a retail price that includes all taxes, and nobody may sell above it (Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules 2011, rule 2(m) and the sale-above-MRP bar, read at source by the labels research and cited in the header of `batches.ts` [C][R labels-and-scanning 3.1]). The tracking spec adds a per-item or per-batch MRP (`mrpAt`), snapshots it on the inward line, and a **ceiling check** at sale: the tax-inclusive unit price `rate x (1 - discount) x (1 + (gst + cess) / 100)` compared with the line's MRP, a Warn or a Block per item (`batches.ts` `mrpCeiling`; rule R26 [S 4.4, 5]). It does **not** price a line from the MRP. This app stores a tax-exclusive rate held to two decimals (`money.ts:132-136`) and adds tax on top, so a bill for an MRP-priced basket cannot be made to equal the sum of MRPs by construction, only by the final whole-rupee round-off (`VoucherScreen.tsx:2691`) hiding paise. A POS needs an **inclusive mode**: the line total is fixed (quantity x MRP less any discount), the tax-exclusive rate is derived backwards from it and stored with enough precision, and the GST snapshot on the line is taken from that derivation. `core/voucherConfig.ts:16-17` records MRP as deliberately not built because it changes the stock and tax arithmetic. [C][S][J]
- **Units and weight:** a unit carries 0 to 4 decimals and compound or alternate units convert on entry (`types.ts:425-436`; typed "5 box" or "24 nos" at `VoucherScreen.tsx:3244-3265`), enough for loose goods by weight and strips against boxes. There is no scale integration beyond the label code: `scale.ts` reads a weighing scale's printed label (item digits plus weight, price or count) through a per-shop profile set by scanning one label; **no Indian scale maker's own layout was found** and the shop's own label is the test [C `scale.ts` header]. A scale that talks over a serial port to the till is not designed (no Web Serial: Appendix B). [C]
- **One sales ledger per bill** (finding in 1.1) means profit and loss shows one sales line however many categories or tax slabs the basket held; GST reports are right because they read the per-line snapshot, but a retailer wanting "Sales: Grocery 5%" and "Sales: Gifts 18%" cannot get it. A restaurant selling food, packaged goods and a service charge on one bill has the same limit. [C][J]

### 1.4 Discount handling and GST correctness on discounts

- **What exists:** a line discount percent column, clamped to 0 to 100, off by default and switched on from F11 or from the voucher's F12 (`VoucherScreen.tsx:1362`, `:3286-3292`; `CompanyCreate.tsx:349`; `voucherConfig.ts:75-81`). The line amount stored is already net of the discount and `discountPct` is kept for display (`types.ts:951`). The GST engine groups the **discounted** line amounts by rate and computes tax on the sum, CGST and SGST each `round2(base x rate / 200)` (`gst.ts:93-108`, `:61-83`). [C]
- **Law:** a discount given before or at the time of supply and recorded on the invoice is left out of the value of supply; a discount given after the supply is excluded only under an agreement made at or before the supply, linked to the invoices, with the recipient reversing the input credit (CGST Act s.15(3)(a) and (b), read in the Act as consolidated on 1 Aug 2021 [W]; CBIC Circular 212/06/2024 on the post-supply case [Ws, not read]). Credit notes are the lawful route after the supply: s.34(1) lets the supplier issue them for a taxable value found to be too high or for goods returned, and s.34(2) has them declared in the return of the month of issue and **not later than a cut-off after the financial year of the supply** (or the annual return, if earlier): **September in the Act as consolidated on 1 Aug 2021, which is the text I read, and 30 November from 1 Oct 2022 as amended by the Finance Act 2022, which I could not read at source and take from the payments study** [W Act copy; R pos-payments-hardware-compliance.md 1.7 and 3.1]. So the engine's behaviour is right for a discount shown on the bill, and a POS must **not** offer "discount after the sale" other than through a credit note. [W][J]
- **What is missing:** a whole-bill percent or rupee discount, coupons, "buy 2 get 1", happy-hour rules, a price-override reason, a discount cap by role (`VoucherScreen.tsx:3287` clamps only to 0..100), and approval. **A discount posted as a Discount ledger in the extras sits outside the taxable value:** the tax rows are prefilled from the item lines (`VoucherScreen.tsx:2033-2038`), so the customer would be charged tax on the pre-discount value. A POS must allocate a bill-level discount **across the lines before tax**; `gst.ts:224-235` `allocate` (largest remainder, exact to the paisa) already exists for exactly this kind of split. [C][J]
- **Free items:** a 100 percent discount gives amount 0 and Enter on a zero amount is refused with "Amount is required!" (`VoucherScreen.tsx:3311-3314`), so a free line cannot be entered in the grid. [C]


### 1.5 Round-off, HSN and B2C details

- **Round-off** is to the whole rupee only, half up, only for a GST item invoice, with a ledger found by name (`VoucherScreen.tsx:2685-2705`); a company without GST gets none, and rounding to 5 or 10 paise is not offered. Bulk Entry copies the same rule (`BulkEntry.tsx:563`). [C]
- **HSN** is snapshotted per line (`types.ts:967`); a missing HSN is reported by GSTR-1 as an error, not at the counter (`gstr1.ts:645`). The B2C half of the HSN table is built (`gstr1.ts:601`). [C]
- **Walk-in place of supply:** a Cash party has no state, `isIntraState` returns true when either state is blank (`gst.ts:18`) and GSTR-1 files the bill under the company's own state (`gstr1.ts:561-567`). Correct for a counter sale in the supplier's state [J]. [C]
- **B2B bill at the counter:** the buyer's GSTIN makes it a B2B invoice; e-invoicing applies to B2B and export supplies of a taxpayer above Rs 5 crore aggregate turnover from 1 Aug 2023 [W: cleartax e-invoicing page], and for a taxpayer at Rs 10 crore or more an invoice older than 30 days can no longer be reported to the IRP from 1 Apr 2025 (GSTN advisory of 5 Nov 2024) [W taxmann]. This app **never generates an IRN**; it imports the portal's results (`CLAUDE.md` "Known limitations" and "Current status"; `docs/research/einvoice-gsp.md`), so a B2B POS bill from a company above the threshold cannot be registered at the counter. **A QR on a B2C invoice is a legal duty only for a supplier above Rs 500 crore aggregate turnover** (Notification 14/2020-Central Tax, in force 1 Dec 2020; Circular 156/12/2021 says so and adds that a person holding a UIN is not a "registered person", so a bill to one is a B2C bill) [W circular read as extracted text]; the QR this app prints is a payment link, not a statutory QR (section 8). [C][W]
- **Invoice number shape:** the portal rule the code checks is 1 to 16 characters of letters, digits, slash and hyphen (`gstr1.ts:313-315`), matching Rule 46(b): up to 16 characters of letters, digits, hyphen and slash, in one series or several, unique for a financial year (read in a reproduction of the Rule, which lists its amendments up to Notification 38/2023) [W gstzen]. A prefix `T1/` + `{fy}` + `/` + a 6-digit width is 15 characters. [C][W]
- **What an unregistered buyer's bill must carry (Rule 46(e) and (f)):** under Rs 50,000 the buyer's name and address (and delivery address and state) only if the buyer asks for them; at Rs 50,000 or more they are required, with the state name and code [W gstzen reproduction of Rule 46, paraphrased]. A walk-in Cash party has none of these fields, and nothing at the counter asks (`Ledger` has address and state, `types.ts:297-298`, but the voucher is saved against the Cash ledger). A POS needs a **bill-level buyer block** (name, phone, address, state) that is required above the Rs 50,000 line and optional below it, stored on the voucher. [W][C][J]
- **The e-commerce-operator rule has no code.** From 1 Jan 2022 the operator, not the restaurant, pays GST on restaurant service supplied through it and issues the invoice; the restaurant reports those supplies and counts them in its turnover (Circular 167/23/2021, read in full by [R pos-restaurant-market.md 4.3]). A grep of `src` for section 9(5), an e-commerce operator or an aggregator finds none (Appendix B), so an aggregator order entered as an ordinary Sales voucher would have the restaurant charge and report tax the operator owes. A new taxability class ("tax paid by the operator under 9(5)": value posted, no output tax posted or printed, the return builders reporting it where the circular says) is a prerequisite of the restaurant mode; the nil, exempt and non-GST reporting slots it would use exist (`gstNil.ts`, `gstr1.ts:670-711`) [R][C]. A restaurant must never print its own tax invoice on such an order. [R][J]
- **A rate that depends on the unit price has no code.** From 22 Sep 2025 apparel, other made-up textile articles and footwear are taxed at 5% when the sale value is not more than Rs 2,500 a piece or pair and at 18% above it; the retail study read this in the PIB release of the 56th Council and says "sale value" is not defined in what it read (secondary sources say tax-exclusive and after an on-invoice discount), so the basis is for a CA to sign [R pos-retail-market.md 6 row 7]. `gstRates.ts` dates a rate by the voucher date and a line can carry an override (`gstOvr`, `gstRates.ts:246`), but a grep of `src/core` for the threshold finds nothing (Appendix B): a POS for apparel needs a per-line rule evaluated on the net unit price, dated so a back-dated bill takes the old rate, and it interacts with every discount (a Rs 3,000 tag less 20 per cent is Rs 2,400 and changes the slab). [R][C]
- **HSN on B2C lines.** Table 12 of GSTR-1 is split into B2B and B2C tabs from the May 2025 period, and the B2B tab is the one consistently reported as mandatory, B2C being optional below the Rs 5 crore threshold (search and vendor summaries disagree on details) [Ws; Tally's page confirms only the split and its date, W]. The app builds both halves (`gstr1.ts:601`, `:1041-1043`); the audit leaves "when is a B2C HSN mandatory" to the sibling statutory work. [Ws][C]

### 1.6 The party model: walk-in, quick-add, credit, loyalty

- **Walk-in:** the seeded `Cash` ledger (`defaults.ts:57`) and the picker offering cash, bank, debtors and creditors (`VoucherScreen.tsx:1965-1971`). No separate "walk-in customer" concept; and ghost recall keyed on the Cash party offers the last bill of **any** customer (`findRecall`, `recall.ts:48-70`, runs a full filter, `PERF.md:176` 52 ms at 250,000). [C]
- **Quick-add by phone:** Alt+C opens the full ledger form in an overlay (`VoucherScreen.tsx:4012-4027`; fields `masters.tsx:354-459` run from name, alias, group, bill-by-bill, credit days through address, state, GSTIN, PAN, contact, phone, e-mail). `Ledger.phone` exists (`types.ts:302`) but the picker matches name and alias only (`VoucherScreen.tsx:1958-1963`), so a customer cannot be found by phone. The phone normaliser from wave 3 (`share.ts:32-66`) is reusable. [C]
- **Credit limit (wave 3):** `creditCheck` (`creditLimit.ts:96-101`) sums the ledger over the whole book (`exposureOf`, `creditLimit.ts:49-56`: one pass, about the cost of `closingBalances`, 76 ms at 250,000 [C `PERF.md:190`]); over the limit asks "why" and keeps the reason on the voucher (`VoucherScreen.tsx:3994-4008`; `creditLimit.ts:108-111`). It is a prompt the cashier answers, not an approval by someone else. [C]
- **Credit sales (khata):** bill-wise ledgers, New Ref named after the voucher number, `followNumber` keeps the name in step if the cloud renumbers (`vchNumbering.ts:266-275`). Collecting dues needs a Receipt with Agst Ref; `openBillsFor` runs a full `outstandingBills` for one party (170 ms at 250,000, `PERF.md:177`, `billwise.ts:99-101`). [C]
- **Loyalty, wallet, gift card, coupons:** nothing (grep, Appendix B). The CRM tables are a **sales pipeline** (Lead, LeadActivity, `types.ts:571-600`) with an optional `ledgerId`, not a customer master. [C]
- **Scale of the customer list:** each customer as a ledger enlarges `st.ledgers` (copied and sorted per change, `VoucherScreen.tsx:1377`), the picker (no virtualisation) and the Day Book's `Array.find` per row (436 ms at 250,000 rows with about 500 ledgers, `PERF.md:248-253`). Retail customer lists run to tens of thousands [J]. A lightweight `customers` table (phone, name, points) with a **ledger only for credit customers** is the shape; the party ledger stays the accounting object (finding 12). [C][J]
- **Loyalty, privacy and the design that fits the engine [J].** A points balance edited by several terminals is a conflict magnet if it is a field on a customer row, so it should be **derived from an append-only table of earn and burn events** (the same reason the restaurant study proposes append-only kitchen tickets [R pos-restaurant-market.md 7.3]); an event carries bill id, terminal and time, and a bill's cancellation voids its events. Capturing a phone number for points or a WhatsApp bill makes the shop a data fiduciary: the DPDP Rules, notified in November 2025, require a plain-language notice stating the purpose, withdrawal of consent at any time, and a response to an access, correction or erasure request within 90 days, on an 18-month phase [W PIB note; the 13 May 2027 date for the notice duties is from law-firm summaries, Ws; the payments study read the same PIB note and sets out the duties at more length, R pos-payments-hardware-compliance.md 4.5]. So the customer table needs a consent flag and date, a purpose, an erase path that keeps the accounting document (a bill is a tax record) and strips the person, and a retention rule; none exists. Whether points are a liability to be provided for is an accounting question for a CA [unverified]. The custom-fields branch lets an administrator add fields to a ledger (a birthday, a segment) [CF], which suits attributes but not balances. [W][CF][J]

### 2.2 How TallyPrime does it [W]

Re-read on 2026-10-25 (the POS page of TallyPrime's help, and the multi-mode payment page and the POS voucher type and voucher class page of the older Tally.ERP 9 Release 6.3 help; the fetch tool answered from the pages, which I did not read line by line). **A POS bill is a separate voucher type under Sales with "Use for POS invoicing" = Yes** (the Tally.ERP 9 class page says the option appears only while the Default Tax Unit is Not Applicable); it also switches on the **POS Register**, a list of those invoices that drills into each one. The party can be set to "Not Applicable" when the buyer is unknown, or a party and buyer details can be shown through F12 options. Items are chosen by scanning into the item field (the barcode lives in the Alias), quantity defaults to 1, and rate, quantity and descriptions can be skipped or modified by F12 options. **Cash is the default mode, with the cash tendered and the balance shown; multi-mode payment** (Alt+I on the TallyPrime page; the Tally.ERP 9 pages name a "P: Multi Mode Payment" button) allocates the bill across payment modes, each with a ledger: **gift vouchers** (ledgers under Sundry Debtors or Sundry Creditors), **credit or debit cards** (a bank ledger under Bank Accounts or Bank OD/OCC, with the card number typed), **cheques** (bank ledger, bank name and cheque number) and **cash** (a ledger under Cash-in-Hand, with tendered and balance). The **multi-mode button is disabled when a POS voucher class is selected**, because the class pre-defines the accepted payment types, each with its ledger, and can pre-define accounting ledgers with a percentage of allocation. The page sets a POS printer and print format through F12 and says the paper size is chosen to suit the printer. Its stated limits: voucher numbers over 16 characters are not exported to e-return formats (a warning can be enabled), **barcode labels cannot be printed from TallyPrime**, and expired and zero-balance batches stay hidden unless F12 options show them. The pages say **nothing about day-end close, returns, or whether the payment amounts must total the invoice** (the Release 6.3 multi-mode page does list "select the godown, sales ledger" among its steps), and **none of the three states which ledgers are debited or credited when the bill is saved** (the multi-mode page refers to an image of the voucher whose content the text does not carry). So what Tally posts for a multi-mode bill is **[unverified]**; the group rules above imply that the tender ledgers are debited directly and the sales ledger credited, with the party ledger absent or nil [J]. A second search for a third-party description of the postings found none. Sources: the three help.tallysolutions.com pages in the list at the end.

[C for the engine facts; J for the ranking.] **Audit position:** build S1 and S2 first (no engine change, correct books at every moment), treat S3 as a measured later step that halves the vouchers for mixed bills once the reader audit is done, keep S4 out of the default because it makes the cash ledger wrong all day (summary posting in 3.4 is S4 at a larger scale), and hold S5 for the outlets where the tender is **not known when the bill is made** (a restaurant check settled after it is printed, a split settled at the till): the restaurant study proposes "a cash and UPI and card clearing ledger per tender" for settlement [R pos-restaurant-market.md 7.3], which is this shape with one clearing ledger per tender, and it needs a CA's view before it is a default, because it moves the cash ledger's meaning until close. Fewer than a minority of bills are mixed-tender in most Indian shops [J, unverified]: the volume cost of S2 is small. **Aggregator receivables are S1:** the party is the aggregator's ledger and the payout clears it bill-wise.

### 2.4 Cash drawer, shift, over/short, Z report

- **Exists:** a `Cash` ledger and `closingBalances` (`accounting.ts:16-26`), a negative-cash warning (only on accounting-grid vouchers, `VoucherScreen.tsx:2450-2464`, not on an item invoice), the Negative Cash Balance exception (`Exceptions.tsx:66-81`), and `ensureLedger` for creating a system ledger by name (`store.ts:759-769`). [C]
- **Does not exist:** shift, float, count by denomination, a variance ledger, a blind close, a Z report, a cash payout or no-sale record (Appendix B). [C]
- **What a close needs from the data model:** the window of a shift. A voucher has no time of day (`types.ts:991-1049`); the change-log row has `ts` but it is overwritten by an alteration and the log is unbounded (3.3). So a bill needs either a stored `at` or a `shiftId`, and the Z report needs an index by shift rather than a scan (3.2). **Expected cash** = float + the shift's net Cash-ledger movement on that terminal's cash ledger; **over/short** = counted minus expected, posted as one voucher against a "Cash short and excess" ledger created through the `ensureLedger` path. One cash ledger per terminal ("Cash - T1") gives drawer-level books with no new engine. [J]
- The bank-deposit slip report that exists (`ui/banking/depositslip.tsx`) is for cheques and is a model for a "bank-in" slip, not a drawer count. [C]
- **What a Z report must carry, and tie to [J].** For one terminal and one shift or day: the first and last bill number of each series and the count (the series' Table 13 row for that window), gross, discounts, net, tax by rate, round-off, takings by tender, refunds and credit notes, cancelled shells (number kept), voids, no-sales and reprints, opening float, expected cash, counted cash and the over or short. The ties are the Phase 28 rule applied to a till: the tender total equals the sum of the bills' party legs; the by-rate figures equal what GSTR-1's B2CS rows would hold for that day; every figure equals the report its drill opens, to the paisa. **A blind close** (the cashier counts before seeing the expected figure) and a **tolerance** above which a manager must sign are policy, not engine.
- **Precedents, as far as I could read them [Ws].** Odoo's POS compares counted with expected cash at closing, warns on a difference and, if the person accepts, books it in a journal entry it labels "Difference at closing PoS session" (an Odoo community answer); an Odoo 18 post describes a "maximum difference" setting as a tolerance (Cybrosys, a third-party blog). ERPNext's POS Closing Entry reconciles the shift by payment mode and, at the same moment, posts the day's sales (3.4). I found no official page that spells out either product's closing entries, so these are shapes to compare with, not specifications. [Ws]

### 2.5 UPI and card references

- The UPI link is `upi://pay?pa=&pn=&am=&tn=&cu=INR` built from the owner's own VPA with the bill amount (`upi.ts:112-130`); **it carries no transaction reference (`tr`)** and the file says so (`upi.ts:18-22`), so a PSP cannot return a payment for reconciliation. It is printed only with the bank block of an A4 invoice (`types.ts:253-256`). The module's own header states that the NPCI specification was read from a third-party copy of the November 2017 draft and must be re-checked before launch (`upi.ts:3-8`). [C]
- Nothing confirms that a UPI payment arrived: the cashier looks at a phone or a soundbox. This is the classic cashier-fraud gap [J]; the controls are a per-tender reference captured on the bill and a **bank-statement match** (the app already imports bank statements: `core/bankStatement.ts`, `core/brsMatch.ts`) rather than a callback. A dynamic QR with confirmation needs a PSP or aggregator API and a server, which the plain-HTTP single-VM backend is not ready for (`HANDOFF.md:89-96`). The payments study lists five ways to know a UPI payment arrived (a PSP callback or poll, an integrated terminal, a soundbox the cashier listens to, a next-day bank statement, an SMS reader) and proposes a tender that carries a confirmation state with a shift-close list of the unconfirmed ones [R pos-payments-hardware-compliance.md 0 item 1, 1.3]. [C][J][R]
- Per-tender reference fields already exist on a voucher entry: `instrumentType` includes `'UPI'` and `'Card'` and `instrument` holds a reference (`types.ts:890-891`, `:904`), captured today by the bank-instrument panel on the accounting grid (`VoucherScreen.tsx:917-1010`). A POS Receipt or tender leg can reuse the fields. A card machine is standalone: the cashier types an approval code or last four digits, and **no full card number should ever be stored or synced** (the sync path is HTTP-only today). That is not only prudence: RBI's circular of 24 June 2022 (RBI/2022-23/77) says no entity in the card payment chain other than card issuers and card networks may store actual card data, any such data held earlier had to be purged, and the storage deadline was extended to 30 September 2022 [W rbi.org.in notification, read]. The allowance to keep the last four digits and the issuer's name for reconciliation comes from the earlier September 2021 circular, which I could not read [Ws]. So the tender record for a card is a bank or acquirer, an approval code and at most the last four digits. [C][W][J]
- **The QR a POS prints is a convenience, not a duty, for the target shops.** The legal dynamic QR on a B2C invoice binds only a supplier above Rs 500 crore aggregate turnover (finding 16); a retailer's static UPI QR is therefore the shop's own choice, and the `tr` reference gap above matters for reconciliation, not for compliance. [W][C]

### 3.3 Dexie, outbox, sync, backup, audit, server

- **Schema:** `vouchers: 'id, cid, date, base'` (`db.ts:124`): no compound `[cid+date]` index, no index on `typeId` or `number`; the company loads by `where('cid')` into memory (`db.ts:573-598`), so a book of N vouchers is N objects in the renderer heap (1.45 KB each [M]). The tracking spec adds no index and no table before its optional T-7b (it argues the engines run over in-memory arrays) [S 3.7]. [C][S]
- **A save** is one IndexedDB transaction with two writes, the document and an outbox row holding the **whole document again** (`db.ts:332-346`); the cost of that transaction at POS rate was not measured **[unverified]**. [C]
- **Outbox:** pushes read **every unsent row** into memory, sort by seq, send 300 per request (`PUSH_BATCH`, `sync.ts:47`), then rewrite those 300 whole rows with `synced: 1` (`sync.ts:619-660`); nothing deletes a synced row (header comment `sync.ts:14-22`). `AuditLog.tsx:101` and `cancelled.tsx:48` run `db.outbox.orderBy('seq').reverse().toArray()` over the entire log before filtering by company: at 1.1 million bills that is about 1.36 GB of rows in memory for the Audit Log and the Cancelled register, the two screens a store owner opens to catch theft [M arithmetic]. The one bounded read is the Quick access "Today" count (`ui/nav/QuickAccess.tsx:74`, `until(r.ts < since)`). [C][M]
- **Pull:** the server returns the whole **tenant** (every company) and every revision of every document in pages of 500 (`sync.js:343-374`, `PULL_PAGE`, `sync.ts:48`; `mobile-backend-readiness.md:70` [R]); a terminal that joins late replays history page by page with `reloadActive` after each (`sync.ts:690-722`, the reload at `:713`). `checkDuplicates` stops looking when more than 5,000 vouchers arrive (`sync.ts:668-670`). The tracking spec adds a byte bound to the push batch and the pull page because a voucher of serials can be 400 KB [S 3.7]; a POS bill is 1.1 KB, so the page-count problem, not the byte problem, is the POS's. [C][R][S]
- **Push on the server:** one writer per tenant at a time (`withTenantLock`, `sync.js:205`), so every terminal of a store serialises on one lock; one `INSERT ... change_log` per batch (`:233`; in the CF branch the push body moved to `pushRun.js:57` [CF]); an SSE nudge to the other devices (`:294`); a correlated `NOT EXISTS` per row on pull (`:359-365`). Single VM, in-memory rate limiter and SSE hub, plain HTTP (`HANDOFF.md:89-96`). **Each bill that asks the cloud for its number is one more call that takes a per-series advisory lock** (`numbering.js` `lockSeries`). [C]
- **Backup:** `JSON.stringify(data, null, 1)` of everything (`autoBackup.ts:205-208`), refused by the desktop bridge above 512 MiB (`main.cjs:35`, `:89`): **292,572 bills of a 5-line shape, about 370,000 of a 4-line one** [M]. A browser string also has an engine length ceiling (V8: about 536 million characters) **[J, unverified]**. Restore is untested (`HANDOFF.md:96`). The desktop app arms a backup at window close and waits at most 15 s for it (`main.cjs:157-175`, `CLOSE_WAIT_MS` at `:158`): a power cut skips it, and IndexedDB's own durability on an abrupt stop is the browser's, not this app's [J]. [C][M]
- **Storage limits are not the wall:** Chromium gives an origin up to 60 per cent of total disk in best-effort mode, Firefox the smaller of 10 per cent of disk or 10 GiB, Safari about 60 per cent; best-effort data can be evicted without a prompt under storage pressure, and `navigator.storage.persist()` asks the browser to exempt the origin (approved or denied on engagement, no guarantee) [W MDN, Storage quotas and eviction criteria]. The app already asks (`data/persist.ts:30-35`) and shows a warning when it is denied. [W][C]
- **Memory:** 722 MB of heap for a 500,000-bill book, about 1.6 GB extrapolated for 1.1 million, before the outbox and React; a low-end PC with 4 GB cannot hold it comfortably **[J]**. [M]
- **Existing escape valve:** Split Company Data carries closing balances, stock and bills into a new company at a chosen date (`core/splitCompany.ts`, `ui/company/SplitCompany.tsx`; `CLAUDE.md` Phase 24); the CF and tracking work extend it to carry variants and cells [CF][S 3.6]. One company per financial year per outlet bounds N to one year of bills. This is a documented feature, not a POS feature, and it needs a POS-friendly, mostly automatic version at 1 April. [C][J]

### 3.4 Voucher-per-bill versus summary posting (the dominant decision)

**Voucher-per-bill (V):** every bill is a Sales voucher. **Summary posting (S):** the bills are kept as non-posting detail documents in a new table, and one Sales voucher per terminal per day (per GST rate and, in a fuller form, per tender) is posted at day close. A hybrid (H) keeps B2B, credit, high-value and returned bills as vouchers and summarises only sub-threshold walk-in cash bills.

**The ERPNext precedent [W docs.frappe.io, POS invoice consolidation; Ws forum threads].** ERPNext keeps each POS Invoice as an intermediate "sub-ledger" record that touches neither stock nor accounts; when the session closes, **one consolidated sales invoice** merges the day's POS invoices and creates the entries (about three to four ledger entries against about three per invoice before), and the stock a session has sold shows in a "Reserved for POS Transactions" figure until then. The forum threads describe the cost of that design: a merge log that maps POS invoices to the consolidated invoice, closing entries that stall or fail on a deleted POS invoice or an unusual tax row, and returns merged into a credit note [Ws]. So the pattern is proven, it was chosen for ledger volume, and its price is a close that can fail and a window in which the books lag the counter. **What it would cost here is larger than there:** the e-way, GSTR-1 Table 13 and cancel machinery here are all per voucher, and a unit chain cannot be summarised.

**A worked example of S, to see what breaks [J].** 300 cash bills in a day at three GST rates fold into one Sales voucher per rate: Dr Cash for the day's total, Cr Sales per rate, Cr CGST and SGST per rate, one Round Off line. GSTR-1 is right (four B2CS rows either way). Table 13 shows one document where 300 were issued. HSN needs one stock line per item sold that day. A customer's return of bill 217 the next morning needs bill 217's detail document to exist, be found by number, and be marked returned, while the summary voucher it fell into has already closed. The books engines are fast; every statutory and audit statement about bills must be rebuilt on the detail table.

### 3.5 The audit's position [J]

1. **V is the system of record.** It is the only mode in which every existing engine, every statutory report and the cancel and numbering machinery work unchanged, and the law requires a numbered invoice for nearly every bill. Its wall (about 250,000 vouchers, 0.5 s an Accept; 2.3 s at 500,000 [M]) is real but reachable around: **lane A** (a hot path: an incremental append instead of copy-and-sort, a cached per-type number, balances and positions kept running, a pull that applies pages without a per-page full reload, a bounded outbox read, a date window on the returns) and **one company per financial year per outlet** (292,000 bills a year at 800 a day is just past today's budget, so lane A must deliver before launch, not after).
2. **S is not an entry mode; it is a close and an archive.** For an outlet above roughly 1,500 bills a day, or for the sub-Rs-200 slice, a day-close summary is the only way to keep the books engines fast [J]; build it **after** V works, as an opt-in lane (section 14, lane S), with Table 13 reading the detail series.
3. **What would change this:** a customer with 3,000 or more bills a day per company and no wish to shard by terminal; a statutory reading that lets all B2C bills of a day be consolidated (the Rule 46 proviso reads against it: Rs 200, unregistered, not asked [W]); or a decision to rebuild the books engines on incremental indexes, which would remove the wall for V and is lane A taken to its end.

## 4. Numbering and Rule 46

- **What Rule 46(b) says [W].** A tax invoice carries a serial number of up to 16 characters, using letters, digits, hyphen and slash, **in one series or several, unique for a financial year** (read in a reproduction of the Rule that lists its amendments to Notification 38/2023; the CBIC page itself fails a certificate check from this machine, and `pos-restaurant-market.md` read the CBIC consolidated PDF as amended to 1 Jun 2021 [R], which agrees). Three consequences for a till: several series per outlet are lawful; every bill needs a number from one of them; and "unique for a financial year" is a property of the **printed string**, not of the counter behind it.
- **The series today:** one counter per (tenant, company, voucher type, numbering window), the window being `''`, the financial year's first day or the month (`numbering.js:7-10`, `:119-128`; `008_voucher_series.sql:18-39`; `vchNumbering.ts:54-56`); prefix, suffix, width and `{fy}` `{yy}` `{yyyy}` `{mm}` tokens are formatted on the client (`store.ts:740-756`); a type has `restart` Never, Yearly or Monthly (`types.ts:531-553`, the field at `:539`). [C]
- **A POS series per terminal = one voucher type per terminal** ("POS T1", prefix `T1/`, `{fy}`, width 6, yearly restart: `T1/26-27/000123`, 15 characters, inside the 16 that Rule 46(b) and the portal check allow [W][C `gstr1.ts:313-315`]). No other device writes a terminal's type, so there is nothing to clash and nothing for the claim machinery to do. Credit notes get their own series per terminal ("R1/..."), receipts (shape S2) theirs. Table 13 groups by type and window, so per-terminal types give clean rows (`gstr1.ts:433-437`); keep a POS type pure, because a type holding other natures is flagged "partial" (`gstr1.ts:459`). The cancelled count of a row is the shells plus the holes of a pure series (`gstr1.ts:459-462`), which is the figure a till needs. [J][C]
- **A trap the code has never guarded: a Monthly restart with no month in the printed number.** The Voucher Type form offers "Restart numbering: Monthly" ("each month", `masters2.tsx:28`, `:77-78`); `nextVchNo` then restarts the counter each month (`store.ts:722-736`) and the server keys the series on `'YYYY-MM'` (`numbering.js:7-10`); but the month reaches the printed string **only if the person typed `{mm}` into the prefix or suffix** (`store.ts:740-756`). Without it April and May of one financial year both issue "INV/000001", which Rule 46(b) forbids and the portal's duplicate check rejects. The only warnings are the IRP-shaped caution at Accept for a B2B invoice in an e-invoicing company (`VoucherScreen.tsx:2441-2449`) and the 16-character check at return time (`gstr1.ts:313-315`). A POS type must refuse Monthly restart unless its format carries `{mm}` (or `{yy}{mm}`), and the general Voucher Type form should say so. A Yearly restart is safe without a token because the number is unique within the year that matters. [C][W][J]
- **Case and the portal.** The payments study reports that since 1 June 2025 the e-invoice portal upper-cases a document number before it computes the IRN, so `inv/1` and `INV/1` are one document, and advises a POS to generate upper-case numbers only and to treat a case-insensitive repeat as a clash [R pos-payments-hardware-compliance.md 3.3, citing einvoice-gsp.md; not re-checked]. The Accept-time caution above already treats numbers case-insensitively for an e-invoice (`VoucherScreen.tsx:2446-2447`); a POS type's format should be upper-case from the start. [R][C]
- **Server numbering is overhead for a series one device owns, and protection for a replaced device.** A signed-in, online Accept makes a round trip of up to 4 s before it falls back to a provisional local number (`vchNumbering.ts:20`, `sync/numbering.ts:88-105`), the breaker then skips the cloud for 30 s (`vchNumbering.ts:28`), and after a long offline stretch **each provisional number is claimed by its own request, oldest first, at most 250 per sync, all before the push** (`vchNumbering.ts:38`, `sync/numbering.ts:153` `reconcileProvisional`): a terminal that traded offline all day at 3,000 bills needs about twelve sync rounds to clear its flags, and every claim takes the series' advisory lock on the server. On patchy shop internet that is a visible stall per bill. `needsNumber` (`vchNumbering.ts:79-82`) is the natural place to say "this type is device-owned, number locally". The cost: **a terminal replaced after a failure must pull the full history before it bills**, because `nextVchNo` reads the vouchers themselves (`store.ts:722-736`) and a fresh device would restart at 1; the cloud's rule that an answer is `max(counter, floor)` (`numbering.js` `decideReserve`) is exactly what protects that case today. So "number locally" trades the stall for a replacement procedure. That is a design decision for lanes A and C, not an assumption. The server also caps a tenant at **20,000 series** (`NUMBER_LIMITS.seriesPerTenant`): terminals x types x windows stays far below it for a chain of hundreds of terminals, but a monthly window multiplies by 12. [C][J]
- **Where a hole can come from, and the guard each needs:**

| Cause | Where | Guard a POS must have |

| Undo of a just-saved voucher deletes it | `undo.ts:118-119` (and the toast, `undo.ts:7-12`) | no Undo on a printed bill: route "take back" to Cancel |

| A failed local write after the cloud answered | `forgetReservation` (`VoucherScreen.tsx:2801`; `numbering.js:236-249`) | reuse |
| Monthly restart with no `{mm}` | `masters2.tsx:77-78`, `store.ts:740-756` | refuse at POS type setup (above) |

- **The working date is the most dangerous default for a counter.** `loadInto` sets the working date to the **last voucher's date** (`store.ts:357`), every save sets it to the saved voucher's date (`store.ts:702`), a new voucher opens on it (`VoucherScreen.tsx:1111`), and F2 changes it (`VoucherScreen.tsx:3008`). A terminal opened on Monday morning with Saturday's last bill would post Monday's sales as Saturday's unless someone notices. [C]

## 5. Hold and recall, returns and exchanges, cancel, undo

- **Hold:** there is no draft concept. The nearest things are the Optional flag (burns a number, section 4), a Sales Order (a numbered order document, no stock or accounting, pending-order tracking: `types.ts:35`, `inventory.ts` `pendingOrders`) and a Memorandum. A held bill needs its own local `posDrafts` table with no number. A cart is typed work, so under the Back build's rules it needs a dirty probe like every dialog that holds typed or scanned work (`state/dirty.ts`; CF Back spec rules B5 and B12 [CF back-nav-spec.md]) or a mouse Back or a rail click will discard it without asking. For a **restaurant** the held bill is the running table, edited by several waiters' devices within seconds; the sync path (0.5 to 4 s debounce, then a full reload per pull, plain HTTP: `realtimeCore.ts:30-31`, `sync.ts:713`) cannot carry that, so live tables need a LAN or low-latency channel of their own, or the append-only ticket model the restaurant study proposes [R pos-restaurant-market.md 7.3, 7.4] **[J]**. [C][CF][R]
- **Recall:** ghost recall is keyed on a party and offers the last voucher of that party (`recall.ts:48-70`); for a Cash party it offers a stranger's basket. A POS recall is a draft by id. [C]
- **Return and exchange:** a Credit Note (`INV_CAPABLE`, `VoucherScreen.tsx:87`) books stock back in (`types.ts:34`, `inventory.ts:25-31`), nets into B2CS with sign -1 (`gstr1.ts:573-577`) and goes to CDNUR only against a B2C **large** invoice (`gstr1.ts:736-744`). The link to the original invoice exists **only through bill-wise "Agst Ref"** (`gstr1.ts:522-541`), and a Cash bill has no bill reference (the Cash ledger is not bill-wise), so a return cannot be tied to its bill and nothing stops a return of more than was sold. The POS writer must store the original voucher's id, and "returned so far per line" is a per-voucher lookup the engines cannot do without an index by id (a linear find today, `store.ts:949`). A return at a cost differing from the original sale cost inflates average cost, as every inward does (`inventory.ts:128-145`). An exchange is a Credit Note and a Sales in one transaction with one net tender. **The statute allows it late:** s.34(1) lets the supplier issue credit notes for goods returned and s.34(2) has them declared in the return of the month of issue, not later than a cut-off after the financial year of the supply or the annual return if earlier (September in the Act as consolidated on 1 Aug 2021, which I read; **30 November** after the Finance Act 2022 changed it from 1 Oct 2022, per the payments study, which also says a credit note must name the original invoice's serial and date, Rule 53(1A) [W; R pos-payments-hardware-compliance.md 1.7 and 3.1; I could not read the amended text]); a POS should therefore refuse a return that would land after that date and say why, and link the credit note to the original bill's number and date. [C][W][J]
- **What the tracking spec adds for returns, and where it is vacuous for a till.** The spec's rule R5 says a return starts from the sale: a serial unit's last event must be a sale by Sales or Delivery Note **to this party**, a batch return defaults to a batch sold to this party, and scanning a unit finds its sale, customer and warranty end [S 5, 7.3]. For a **walk-in the party is the Cash ledger on every bill**, so "to this party" matches every Cash sale and checks nothing about the person; the unit index still finds the exact sale of an IMEI, which is the link a serial item needs, but a batch or an untracked item needs the POS's own `originalBillId`. A unit sold before a Split Company Data cut is "unknown" and is a Warn, not a Block [S 7.3, 4.6]. [S][J]
- **Cancel (the lawful void):** Alt+X asks a reason of at least 3 letters (`reasonText.ts:12-13`, `:50-53`) then a second question with the safe answer armed, then saves the shell, which keeps number and date, posts nothing and puts stock back (`voucherCancel.ts:1-14`, `:180-214`); it is offered from an alteration screen, the Day Book and the registers (`useCancelVoucher.tsx:27-29`). It is a write of an existing voucher, so the write guard treats it as an **alter** (`access.ts:359-360`): granting a cashier "cancel" grants "edit any saved bill", and there is no separate right (`types.ts:681`). The server refuses a stale write that would un-cancel a shell (`conflict.js:51-62`, `:224-228`; deployed 2026-10-23, not exercised on the VM [C CLAUDE.md]). [C]
- **Edit after print:** an alteration of a printed, paid bill is allowed for anyone holding the alter action; the e-invoice guard exists only for a voucher that holds an IRN (`alterGuardIrn`, `VoucherScreen.tsx:2783-2793`). For a counter, post-print changes should be cancel-and-reissue only. [C][J]

## 6. Stock at the counter: races, negative stock, batches, serials, variants

- **Stock is derived from the log, never reserved.** A sale reads the local position (`VoucherScreen.tsx:1371`, `:2490-2505`). Two terminals offline each see the last unit and each sell it; after sync the item is -1. The sync conflict machinery compares versions of the **same document** and flags duplicate voucher numbers and restores (`syncGuard.ts:294` `DupGroup`, `:421` `restoreConfirmed`), so two different bills raise **no conflict at all**. Negative stock then shows only in the Exceptions report as at a period end (`Exceptions.tsx:31-64`). [C]
- **Negative stock is a prompt on every bill.** `doAccept` adds "will go NEGATIVE ... Continue anyway ?" to the warnings and stops for a Yes (`VoucherScreen.tsx:2490-2510`), even on Ctrl+A (a direct Accept still stops when `warns` is not empty, `:2507-2511`). Shops often sell before the purchase is entered; the POS needs "allow, silently, and report" as a setting. The tracking design makes the same choice per item: an upgraded batch item keeps `strict: false`, under which a cell going below zero is only a Warn, and a new tracked item is strict and blocks unless an operator holding the Inventory right overrides with a reason [S 2.3, 5 rules R10, R23]. [C][S]
- **Terminals and locations:** a line carries a `godownId` (`types.ts:946`); a voucher's default is the Main location (`VoucherScreen.tsx:1141-1145`); transfers are Stock Journals. One godown per counter or outlet is the cleanest isolation and matches Tally's POS, whose invoice takes a godown with its sales ledger (the Tally.ERP 9 Release 6.3 multi-mode page lists "select the godown, sales ledger" among the steps; the TallyPrime POS page does not discuss godowns) [W]. [C][J]
- **Batches today (the wired app):** an item can be "maintained in batches" (`types.ts:520`); a line holds a batch name and manufacturing and expiry dates (`types.ts:957-959`); the entry is a modal where the cashier **types** the batch name and expiry (`BatchPrompt`, `VoucherScreen.tsx:882-915`, opened only on Alt+B). There is no list of available batches, no first-expiring-first-out pick, no refusal of an expired batch, no per-batch rate or MRP, and the engine keys the per-batch quantity on the batch name alone, not on (godown, batch) (`inventory.ts:14`, `:141-145`). [C]
- **What the tracking design gives a counter (merged as engines, not wired) [S][C headers].** (1) **One stock key**: a cell is `(godown, batch, variant)`, a serial is a unit identity under a cell, and `byGodown`, the legacy `byBatch` and CF's `byVariant` stay as projections (`core/stockCell.ts`; T1). (2) **A scan-to-bill allocation**: a scanned batch code or an outward line proposes cells first-expiry-first-out (or oldest first), skipping expired, held and short-dated stock, rolling a short batch into the next with a visible split; "auto-allocate scans" is on by default; the chosen batch, expiry and MRP stay on the row as a chip and are never silent (`batches.ts` `proposeAllocation`; T7, T9 [S 4.4, 6.4, 14.5]). (3) **Rules**: R10 a cell never goes below zero, R11 expired stock is not sold, R13 a held or recalled batch is not issuable, R26 the MRP ceiling, R27 a sale cannot create a new batch, R3 an issued unit must be in stock here, R1 a unit is unique; each Block, Warn or Inform, with an override that **checks the operator's right before it asks for the reason** and stores the reason on the voucher (`Voucher.trackOverrides`) [S 5]. (4) **Costing stays per item** (Avg. Cost by default; `Specific` per-unit is an opt-in with an AS 2 caution, never the Mobile preset's default) [S T6]. (5) **Units**: a serial item's quantity IS the unit count, capped at 5,000 so a scanned 15-digit IMEI in Quantity is refused; an IMEI is unique across the company, a dual-SIM unit is one unit with two identifiers, an outward row stores the primary one [S T10, T11]. [S]
- **What it does not give, for a POS.** (a) **No manager approval at the counter**: an override needs the *current operator* to hold the right; a cashier who lacks it cannot be overridden by a manager standing beside the till without signing in as that manager (a PIN check of 260 ms or more per switch, section 9). A second-identity approval step is a POS design item. (b) **No cross-terminal check for batch quantities.** The honest-guarantee table is explicit: one open window is enforced; two online devices are stopped **only for serial units and only if the optional reserve slice T-9b is built**; two devices with one offline are **detected at sync, for units, by a server table `unit_events` and a new mismatch kind `duplicate-serial`**, and nothing in the spec checks that two counters did not both sell the last strip of one batch [S 10.1 to 10.3]. A batch cell that goes negative through two offline counters appears only as a Data Check kind and in a window's own R7 check. For a pharmacy with several counters the policy has to be stated: allow, flag and reconcile. (c) **A POS bill is not yet a place the rules run**: R10 and the others run in `doAccept` and in a write-funnel guard in `putDoc` for a company with `features.tracking` [S 5 callers 1 and 2]; a POS writer that calls `store.saveVoucher` goes through the funnel, but the allocation and the chip are screen features, so the POS screen must build its own allocation step on the same `proposeAllocation`. [S][J]
- **Variants (size and colour): built in the custom-fields branch, not on `main`.** Revision 1 could not establish whether a variants feature existed; it does, as **CF-9**: a line-level custom field marked "tracks stock" makes `(item, variant)` a stock position, pooled costing per item, a Physical Stock count made by variant (a `countPart` group merged once in `stockMovements`), Opening by variant, the Stock by field screen, and a per-(item, variant) negative-stock warning and "in stock N" hint on the voucher screen; per-variant costing (CF-9e) and variant-aware manufacture (CF-9f) are **not** built [CF notes 2, `core/variantCore.ts`, `core/stockByField.ts`, `core/variantEntry.ts`]. The variant is read from the line's own `udf` and never stored on the line [S Appendix R item 6]. For an apparel counter that means: scan a variant barcode, the registry resolves it to `(item, VariantKey)` by option ids so a renamed colour keeps its printed code [S 14.4], and the bill line carries the variant as a line field. The spec widens CF-9's shipped shape rather than asking CF to change it (T-0b), gated on CF-9a being committed, reviewed and green on a rebased `main`: **it is now committed in the CF branch, not on `main`**. [CF][S]
- **Serials and IMEI (mobile and electronics):** the unit state is a derived, rebuildable index over the lines' `serials`, with no table and no Dexie bump (T3); a counter's flow is "scan the box, the unit must be in stock here, the line's quantity is the count, the invoice prints the IMEI (both for a dual SIM) and the warranty end derived from the item's rule" [S 7.2]. A sale of a unit another counter already sold is blocked inside one window and detected at sync across devices (T18). **A serial or IMEI bill can never be summarised** (3.4). [S]
- **Pharmacy requirements, as the sibling documents read the law [R pos-pharma-market.md 3.8, 3.9; S T34].** The tracking spec adds the maker and the schedule (none, C, H, H1, X) to the item and prints the manufacturer on a scheduled drug's line, and **says plainly that an invoice is not the record Drugs Rules 65(3) require**: the H1 register wants the prescriber's name and address, the patient's name, the drug and quantity at the time of supply, kept for three years, and a pharmacist's signature, none of which Comsky captures (spec question 26, default "not in v1") [S 18, 17]. None of prescriber, patient or the H1 register has a field today (a bill's only free text is its narration, `types.ts:1048`). A pharmacy POS therefore needs a counter capture step and a register screen that the spec deliberately leaves out: the biggest vertical gap in the tracking design. [S][R][C]
- **Restaurants:** stock effect at sale is nil in the restaurant study's v1, ingredient depletion by recipe at day-end; `StockItem.bom` exists (`types.ts:523`) and is read only by the Manufacturing screen, Data Check and the Tally exporter (grep of `.bom`), so nothing consumes a recipe at sale time [C][R pos-restaurant-market.md 0.2 item 10]. [C][R]

## 7. Keyboard-first versus touch, and a kiosk Electron

- **The key stack:** one `keydown` listener on `window`, a last-in-first-out stack of handlers, an optional translation table for a person's own keys (`keys.ts:11-36`, `:40-100`); screen handlers register with `useScreenKeys`; every chord is spelled once in `core/shortcutRegistry.ts` (reserved list `:10`, rebindable rows `:175`). A POS screen mounts as a normal screen and gets the stack for free. **`keys.ts` is a one-writer file for two other builds**: the Back build adds `runEscape` (+17 lines [CF `keys.ts`]) and the tracking build's scan wiring (T-5b) adds one capture-phase listener and an exported `dispatchKey` there, after Back [S 14.3, 15.1]. A POS adds nothing to it. [C][CF][S]
- **A barcode scanner is a burst of keys ending in Enter**, and the burst is now handled in design and in pure code (`core/scanInput.ts`, section 1.3): detection by event timestamp, an opt-in per-device layer that changes nothing when off, scan targets that take a scan as one change, and a hold of the first key (35 ms by default) **only in a bare-key context** (focus on the page body or a non-interactive container: a menu, a report, the Accept bar) [S T25, 14.3]. For a POS that sets one design rule: **keep every POS control inside a scan target or a text field, or on an F-key or Alt chord, and put no bare-letter hotkey on the counter screen**, so the hold never delays a cashier and a scan never fires a hotkey. A counter turns the layer on in Settings > Devices > Scanner, whose setup scan measures the shop's own scanner; **none of it has run against a real scanner** [S 14.7, 18]. [S][C][J]
- **The shell:** a three-column grid, left rail | screen | F-key rail (`styles.css:633`, `:642`; `AppShell.tsx:78-82`, rail 212px in `styles/navtree.css:7`), with a measured floor of 938px: at 768 and 390px the page scrolls sideways (`docs/design/ux-analysis-2026-10.md` section 4.12 [R]). "Hide menu" collapses only the left column. A touch POS (1024x768, 1280x800, a phone) needs **its own root** that replaces the shell on a terminal in POS mode, not a screen inside it. The only touch rule in the stylesheets is `@media (hover: none)` in `navtree.css:176`; the rail buttons are clickable but the entry grid is keyboard inputs. The Back control is mounted in the top bar by one import (`widgets.tsx`) [CF]; a POS root outside the shell does not carry it, which is right for a till, but a POS *screen inside* the shell would have to register its cart with the dirty probes. [C][R][CF]
- **Electron:** one 1280x820 window, minimum 960x600, no menu (`main.cjs:179-191`, `:209`), `contextIsolation` on and Node integration off (`:188-190`); a grep finds no `kiosk`, `fullscreen`, `requestSingleInstanceLock`, `setLoginItemSettings` or `powerSaveBlocker` (Appendix B). The platform has what a counter needs: a window can be put in kiosk mode (`win.setKiosk(flag)`, `win.kiosk`), the app can refuse a second instance (`app.requestSingleInstanceLock()` returns false in the second process, and a `second-instance` event lets the first bring its window forward), and it can start at login on Windows and macOS (`app.setLoginItemSettings`) [W Electron v31 docs, `browser-window.md` and `app.md`, read via the fetch tool]. **The single-instance lock is not a nicety here**: two POS windows on one machine share one IndexedDB but each holds its own in-memory copy of the book, and the tracking spec states that the app has no cross-window data coordination and that its stock rules are therefore guaranteed **per open window**, not per device [S 5, 10.1]. The bridge exposes backup, `pdf.save` and `share.open` only (`preload.cjs:6-43`), and each IPC is a pure validator in `exportBridge.cjs` because "the renderer is not trusted" (`exportBridge.cjs:8-16`), tested by `exportbridge.assert.ts`: every new device IPC (silent print, printer list, drawer) must follow that pattern, and the tracking spec's label bridge is the first designed after it [S 13.6]. Closing the window arms a backup and waits at most 15 s for it (`main.cjs:157-175`), which suits a desk and not a till that is switched off at the wall. [C][W][S]
- **Platforms:** the desktop build targets Windows and Mac (`CLAUDE.md` "Run / build"; Linux is not built); an Android tablet is a browser or installed web app, which needs HTTPS: the live site is HTTP only and the camera, service workers, Web Share, Barcode Detection and install need a secure context (`HANDOFF.md:90`; `mobile-personas-and-jobs.md:108` [R]). The tracking spec's camera scanning (T-10) is gated on TLS and is explicitly "for a phone doing a stock-take or a price lookup, **not** a counter doing 300 bills a day" [S 14.8]. The mobile research lists the counter-staff persona (S3) with the same needs: barcode and fast billing, held bills, price override with approval, shift close, reprint, on shop Wi-Fi with power cuts (`mobile-personas-and-jobs.md:304`, `:357` [R]). `docs/design/mobile-app-spec.md` and a `mobile/` folder do not exist (checked 2026-10-25), so there is no mobile plan to read, only the research. [C][R][S]

## 8. The receipt path: print, UPI QR, WhatsApp

- **Print today:** the print host is built and sent to `window.print()` (`reportTools.ts:257-279`; `PrintVoucher.tsx:608`), so every receipt is an operating-system print dialog. The sheet geometry knows A4, A5 and Letter only (`types.ts:186`, `printConfig.ts:33`; the bridge's own list `exportBridge.cjs:24`), each a named `@page` (`printConfig.ts:75-79`). The narrowest layout is the A5 compact set under 160 mm of content width (`printConfig.ts:64-66`); an 80 mm roll prints about 72 mm and a 58 mm roll about 48 mm [J]. A POS type can set `printAfterSave` (`types.ts:543`), which pushes the print screen after Accept (`VoucherScreen.tsx:2826-2829`), then P, then the OS dialog: at least three more actions per bill. [C]
- **What the tracking spec already designs, and what it leaves for a receipt [S 13.6, C `electron/labelLang.cjs`].** Four print paths for labels, in build order: **P1** the browser dialog (an injected `@page` for a roll, each label a page), **P2** Electron driver print to a **listed** printer (silent, a named `deviceName`, a custom `pageSize`), **P3** a raw bitmap job in TSPL (ZPL refused until its graphic-field command is read and proven) over TCP 9100 to an allow-listed printer or through the Windows spooler's RAW type via a bundled, hash-checked PowerShell script, and **P4** WebUSB or Web Serial after TLS. The renderer is untrusted: it sends a structured job, never a command, a host or a path, and the main process builds the bytes. **`labelLang.cjs` is on `main` and emits TSPL only; a grep of `src` and `docs/design` for ESC/POS finds nothing** (Appendix B). A receipt printer speaks ESC/POS (text, QR, images, the cutter and the **cash-drawer kick**) [R labels-and-scanning.md 2, W node-thermal-printer, as that research read it], so: (i) **P2 serves a 58 or 80 mm receipt only if the one-day printed spike proves it**: the spec itself records that Electron 31's documentation does not state the units of the `pageSize` object or whether `print()` honours a CSS `@page` size, and that the browser-dialog path is "unproven until the spike has run" on real printers [S 13.6, 18]; (ii) **an ESC/POS raw path and a drawer pulse are new work**, naturally one more `kind` of the same structured job (lines, a QR payload, a cut, a pulse; never a command string), written beside `labelBridge.cjs` (the payments and hardware study proposes exactly this: `receipt.print`, `drawer.open`, `display.*` and `scale.*` handlers, an `escposLang.cjs` twin of `labelLang.cjs`, a drawer event log, and a note that a receipt, unlike a label, is printed on every bill, so a PowerShell process per job and a print that blocks Accept are both wrong [R pos-payments-hardware-compliance.md 2.6]) and wired by the same two small hunks in `main.cjs` and `preload.cjs` that T-7a and Back's B-6 already contend for [S 15.1]; (iii) a receipt is not a label: automatic height, a narrow tax-by-rate summary, the UPI QR, a thermal font. Electron's own pages say `webContents.print` takes `silent`, `deviceName` and a `pageSize` and that `getPrintersAsync()` lists the printers, the units and `@page` questions aside [W Electron v31 `web-contents.md`, via the spec]. A browser alone cannot print silently without a launch flag **[unverified: no page read]**. [S][C][W]
- **A 58/80 mm receipt model [J].** Items with quantity, rate and amount; tax shown per rate (the engine already splits per real slab, `gst.ts:273-346`), the round-off, the invoice number and date, the shop's GSTIN and, for a restaurant, its FSSAI number (the restaurant study reads the FSSAI order as requiring the licence number on bills [R pos-restaurant-market.md 4.7]); the buyer block of 1.5 when required; the QR; a footer the owner chose (revision 1's gotcha stands: never print legal wording or bank details the owner did not choose, `CLAUDE.md` Phase 20-21). New work in `print/` and `printConfig.ts` (`PaperSize` is a closed union in `types.ts:186`). [C][J]
- **UPI QR:** `core/qr.ts` and `ui/print/QrCode.tsx` draw any string as an SVG QR; the payment link carries the bill amount but no reference (2.5). On a receipt the QR must print without the bank block it is tied to on invoices (`types.ts:253-256`). It is a convenience, not a duty, below Rs 500 crore turnover (finding 16). [C][W]
- **WhatsApp:** `waLink` opens `https://wa.me/<digits>?text=...` in the person's own app and nothing is sent by this app (`share.ts:1-4`, `:127-131`); the text is capped at 3,500 characters (`share.ts:108`); the desktop bridge accepts only that link shape (`exportBridge.cjs:146-176`). The message is a **letter** ("Dear ..., Invoice N dated ... for Rs ...", `share.ts:173-185`), not an itemised receipt, and a wa.me link cannot attach a file. Sending a PDF or a hosted e-bill link needs the WhatsApp Business platform (a server, a Meta account, approved templates) or a hosted bill page, neither of which exists **[J]**. [C]
- **Not present:** customer-facing display, kitchen printer routing, a "DUPLICATE" mark on reprints, a reprint count. [C]

## 9. Operators, rights, and what the server does not enforce

- **What exists:** security is opt-in per company (`types.ts:698-706`); a Role has rights areas, optional `voucherBases`, `voucherActions` (create, alter, delete), a date window; the company has a period lock (`types.ts:683-706`; `access.ts:249-279`); PIN sign-in (PBKDF2, 310,000 iterations; where WebCrypto is missing, as on the plain-HTTP site, a pure-JS fallback runs: **260 ms for one check on this machine [M]**, a second or more on a low-end PC [J]: `vault.ts:42-48`, `:172-233`); every change-log row is stamped with the operator (`db.ts:341-345`). The write guard runs in `putDoc` (`db.ts:332-336`, `store.ts:931-961`). [C][M]
- **What a counter needs and the model lacks:**

| Need | Today |
|---|---|
| a cashier may sell but not open the Day Book, cash book or outstandings | impossible: the voucher right is the Accounting area, which also governs `daybook`, `cashBankBook`, `outstandings`, `ledgerVouchers` (`access.ts:129-133`); a blind cash close cannot be built on it |
| rights per voucher TYPE (the POS type only) | `voucherBases` is by base: allowing Sales allows the ordinary Sales screen and every Sales type (`access.ts:262-264`) |
| void, refund, price override, discount cap, no-sale, reprint | no such rights (`types.ts:681`); cancel counts as alter (5) |
| a manager approves on the spot | none; the credit-limit reason is typed by the cashier (`VoucherScreen.tsx:3994-4008`) |
| quick cashier switch | sign-in is a gate screen (`access.ts:181-185`), one PIN check of 260 ms or more |
| detective controls | the Audit Log and the Cancelled register load the whole outbox (`AuditLog.tsx:101`, `cancelled.tsx:48`); Exceptions is as at a period end (`Exceptions.tsx:31`) |

- **Server:** `/sync/push` checks that the subscription allows syncing (`canSync`, a 402 when a trial has ended: `sync.js:184-186`), that each change has a table name of the right shape, an op and an id (`sync.js:188-192`; `TABLE_NAME` is a pattern, not a list, and `conflict.js:74-91` says the route deliberately does **not** restrict to the known tables, because a newer client may sync a table an older server has not heard of and refusing would strand its outbox). Since wave 3 and the CF branch the server does read payloads, but only to apply **keep rules** (a cancelled voucher stays cancelled, `conflict.js:51-62`; fields an older client omits are put back, `UNVERSIONED_PROTECTED` and the CF branch's capability-keyed `CAP_KEPT` [C][CF]); it never judges who may write what. The membership role is owner, admin or member and is used only for the mismatch report (`sync.js:59-61`, `:380-395`; `002_p2.sql:12-15`). So **any member's token can write any voucher or any role**, and a cashier terminal holds that token in the browser's storage (`sync.ts` `TOKEN_KEY`). `access.ts:12-14` says the model is "a client-side control for people sharing a screen, not a data-security boundary". The mobile research reached the same conclusion and proposes a server-side voucher-type allow-list and a second identity per tenant (`mobile-backend-readiness.md:24`, `:66`, `:249` [R]); a POS needs the same, plus a per-terminal credential. [C][CF][R]
- **Consequence:** until the server enforces, POS theft prevention is **detective**: every void, discount, return, no-sale and reprint is an event with operator, terminal and time, a shift report that shows them, and an exceptions list the owner reads. Drawer opens and reprints are not data writes today, so they need their own event log. [J]

## 10. Multi-store, reports and the Dashboard, Settings and feature switches

### 10.1 Multi-store

A company is one set of books with one GSTIN (`types.ts:121`); every row carries `cid`. A godown is a location inside a company (`types.ts:454-460`). A store chain in several states needs one company per GSTIN; outlets in one state can share a company with a godown, a series prefix and a cash ledger each. There is **no outlet dimension on the voucher** (godown lives on the lines; cost-centre allocations are not asked on invoices, `custom-fields-code-audit.md` finding 10 [R]), no consolidated multi-company report (grep, Appendix B), and the device pulls the whole tenant, every company (`mobile-backend-readiness.md:70` [R]). **The custom-fields branch changes this, and revision 1 undersold it.** It adds a **voucher-header field** (a strip under Narration) and a family of reports that read any such field: **Summary by field** (any voucher family), **Income and expense by field** (the ledger postings, tied to the Profit and Loss) and **Bills and Balances by field**. A header field on the POS voucher type (an Outlet, a Terminal, a Shift) is the `own` source of Income and expense by field, the spec's own example "Profit by Zone, by Branch, by Business line", and the report **computes its tie to the Profit and Loss and shows the difference, which must be 0.00** (custom-fields spec decisions CF2 and CF22 and its use case 8 [CF custom-fields-spec.md]; [CF `core/fieldPl.ts` header]). Limits, stated by the same code: the change in stock cannot be attributed by a header field and stays one line, "Change in stock (not attributed)", so per-outlet cost of goods still comes from godown-based stock reports; a field never reaches a GST return or moves stock, so it cannot replace the per-terminal series (4), the per-terminal cash ledger (2.4) or one GSTIN per company; and it exists only after that branch merges. The POS writer must write the header through the branch's carry funnel and entry rules, or a mandatory field would be bypassed at this door [CF `voucherFields.ts`, `entryPolicy.ts`]. [C][CF][J]

## 14. The build: files grouped into parallel-buildable lanes

## 15. The decisions that dominate

1. **Voucher-per-bill or summary posting** (3.4, 3.5). My position: voucher-per-bill with lane A and one company per financial year per outlet; summary posting later and opt-in, for the sub-Rs-200 slice and for outlets above about 1,500 bills a day. **The owner's four numbers decide it:** bills per day per outlet at the target customers, the share that are B2B, the share on credit, and the share under Rs 200.
2. **Tender shape** (2.3): S1 and S2 now, S3 only after a reader audit of `gst.ts`, `accounting.ts` and the print and e-invoice models; S5 (a clearing ledger settled per shift) only for outlets that cannot know the tender when the bill is made, and only after a CA has seen it; S4 never the default.
3. **Own POS root and writer, or a mode of the voucher screen.** My position: own, because the voucher screen is 4,118 lines being edited by three other builds (finding 14) and carries a walk this module must not inherit; the writer is built beside `bulkPlan.ts` once the CF branch merges.
4. **Series per terminal, numbered locally or by the cloud** (4): locally removes a 4 s stall and a claim storm but needs a procedure for a replaced terminal; by the cloud keeps the replacement safe and costs a call per bill. Either way a POS type **refuses a Monthly restart without `{mm}`**.
5. **Customers:** a light table with a consent flag and a ledger only for credit customers; loyalty points as append-only events (1.6). How much loyalty belongs in v1 given the DPDP duties that start in 2027 is a product call.
6. **Hold** is a local draft table; **restaurant live tables** need a decision between append-only tickets over today's sync and an on-premises channel (5).
7. **Stock policy:** allow negative silently and report; the tracking design checks units across devices and batches only per window, so state the policy for two counters selling the last strip (6).
8. **Rights:** what the server will enforce, the per-terminal credential, and a manager-approval step the tracking spec does not design (9, 6).
9. **Platform order:** Windows desktop first; Android needs HTTPS and a hosting decision first (7).
10. **A real-time IRN path** for B2B bills above the e-invoice threshold (1.5), a separate research item (`docs/research/einvoice-gsp.md`).
11. **The order against the other builds.** Does the CF branch merge and deploy first (the backend half must go before the SPA [CF notes 6]), and does the tracking lane's T-0b (cells in `stockPositions`) go before a POS wires batch or variant stock? A POS can start its pure lanes now; lanes A and C need a base that contains the CF branch (12, 14).
12. **One Dexie bump and one backup schema number, shared with T-7b and the CF and tracking schemas** (12), and **where company POS policy lives**: `Company.pos` with its own older-client defence, or a sibling section of `entryPolicy` (10.3).
13. **Which receipt printers and drawers first**, and the printed spike that decides whether the browser path is enough (8).
14. **The restaurant's operator-pays tax class and service-charge posture** need a CA's sign-off before the restaurant mode ships (`pos-restaurant-market.md` 4.10 lists ten items [R]); **pharmacy needs a counter capture of prescriber and patient** that the tracking spec leaves out on purpose.

## 16. What this audit did not verify

- **No browser or Electron timing.** The O(N) rows are pure-engine time on a busy M4 (load 14 to 20 on 10 cores) from the Appendix C script and from `PERF.md`; none is time-to-paint, and the IndexedDB read and write cost at POS rate is unmeasured. Each size was run three times (the GSTR-1 build twice); some figures moved by a factor of two between runs under load, which is why ranges are shown. **I did not look for the cause of the faster-than-linear growth** of `stockPositions` and `buildGstr1`, and no figure above 500,000 bills is claimed.
- **One synthetic shape.** Every bill in Appendix C is a cash sale of 1 to 8 lines against the one Cash ledger, with no batch, no variant, no bill-wise and no returns; a real shop's skew, its B2B share and its credit sales change the figures, and a batch-tracked or variant-tracked book changes `stockPositions` again (the CF gate measures that at 1.17 to 1.67 times the plain call at 250,000 vouchers [CF notes 1]).
- **Tally's posted entries** for a multi-mode POS bill: the three help pages, re-read this session, are silent, and a search found no third-party description.
- **Statutory texts, exactly what was and was not read.** Read at source: CGST Act sections 15, 31 and 34 (the Act as consolidated on 1 Aug 2021: **later amendments are not in that copy, and s.34(2)'s cut-off is one that did change**), Circular 156/12/2021 (as extracted text), RBI circular RBI/2022-23/77, the PIB note on the DPDP Rules. Read in a **reproduction**: Rule 46 and its provisos (the CBIC page fails a certificate check from this machine; the restaurant study read the CBIC consolidated PDF as amended to 1 Jun 2021 and agrees [R]) and the B2C QR page. **Not read:** Notification 14/2020 itself (the restaurant study read it [R]), the DPDP Rules text (the 13 May 2027 date for the notice duties is from law-firm summaries), RBI's September 2021 circular (the last-four-digits allowance), CBIC Circular 212/06/2024, the HSN B2C optionality and Table 13 timing (search, vendor and secondary sources disagree on dates). **Restaurant GST, composition, service charge, FSSAI and Drugs and Cosmetics detail are not re-audited**: `pos-restaurant-market.md` section 4 and `pos-pharma-market.md` sections 3.8 and 3.9 carry them, and both were being revised in the working tree while this was written.
- **No hardware.** Printers, scanners, drawers, scales, card terminals and soundboxes were not touched; every statement about them is code-side or a spec's hypothesis. The scan layer and the label bridge were never run against a scanner or a printer [S 18].
- **No competitor product** was used; what Petpooja, Marg, LogicERP, GoFrugal, ERPNext or Odoo do is what their own pages say, as the sibling research and this audit read them, and the Odoo and ERPNext closing behaviour here is from one official ERPNext page and community and third-party summaries [Ws].
- **The CF branch and the tracking engines** were read, not run: I ran none of their suites. Their line numbers are those of `2517916` and `5aee765` and move when the branch is merged.
- **The browser string ceiling** for the backup, the Electron DevTools exposure and the single-instance behaviour on a particular OS are from general knowledge or documentation, not a test.
- **`docs/design/mobile-app-spec.md` does not exist**, so there is no mobile plan to audit against.
