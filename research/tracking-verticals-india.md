## 1. The answer on one page

1. **India needs four different "units of tracking", not one**: the **serial** (one physical piece with its own identifier: IMEI, appliance serial, jewellery tag), the **batch** (a lot of one item that shares an expiry, an MRP and a statutory record), the **variant** (a sellable style, size and colour with its own barcode and shared cost) and, cheaply, the **pack** (a barcode that stands for N pieces). Each vertical in section 4 is one of these, sometimes two. Tally has one (batch), documents "use the IMEI as the batch name" for the second [Ws TAL-IMEI] and treats the other two as workarounds.
2. **Batch in India is mostly an MRP, expiry and statutory-record device, not a genealogy device.** The Drugs Rules put the batch number and expiry into the pharmacy's bill for Schedule H and H1 supplies and the batch number into every wholesale and retail purchase record and wholesale sale memo [W DR Rule 65]. The Packaged Commodities Rules let a revised MRP apply to packs of a given month, so one item can lawfully sit in stock at two MRPs [W LM Rule 18]. So **a batch must carry MRP and the sale price must be able to follow the batch.** Comsky's batch today carries a name, an expiry and nothing else [C types.ts:953-955, inventory.ts:14].
3. **What Busy's customers like is concrete and copyable** [W BUSY-BAT, BUSY-SER, BUSY-VAR, BUSY-FAQ44]: batch number, quantity, manufacturing date, expiry date or expiry days, batch-wise MRP and sale price, an advance-warning period, a configurable action on expired batches, duplicate-batch and negative-stock warnings, a batch picker at sale or a scan, serials with per-serial MRP and price, up to ten descriptions, auto or manual numbering, purchase, sale and installation warranty, up to five variant parameters with MRP, price and barcode per variant, a scheme master, and reports (Batch-wise Stock, Expiring Batches, Batch On Account, Serial No.-wise Stock, parameterised stock, barcode-number stock). **Busy does not claim first-expiry-first-out batch selection** on the page I read [W BUSY-BAT]: that is a place to be better. Busy gates batch details by edition (the basic edition lacks them) [W BUSY-FAQ44].
4. **Tally's documented position:** batch (name, manufacturing date, expiry, cost differences) with Batch Summary, Batch Vouchers and Stock Ageing; no per-batch MRP in the pages I read [W TAL-BATCH]; **no serial numbers** (the IMEI trick makes one batch per unit, with "closing quantity 1 means unsold" as the report) [Ws TAL-IMEI]; **a barcode is the item's Alias** (the blog says up to 32 characters, the help article describes a scan recording a 16-digit number), the blog says you need a separate generator and software to print, and a help article says a barcode cannot be printed as a label in TallyPrime [W TAL-BAR; Ws TAL-HELP]; its FMCG guide mentions neither batch nor MRP [W TAL-FMCG]. **The release notes of TallyPrime 7.0, 7.1 (20 May 2026) and 7.2 (12 Oct 2026) list no batch, serial, barcode, label or expiry item**; the one MRP item is GST on a tax-inclusive MRP for tobacco goods [W TAL-REL].
5. **The GST e-invoice schema already has a home for all of it**: barcode (3 to 30 characters), product serial number (1 to 20), batch name (3 to 20) with expiry and warranty dates, name and value attributes (1 to 100 each), country of origin and free quantity; an invoice holds 1 to **1,000** lines [W NIC; W EINV]. Comsky emits only the batch name and expiry [C `einvoice.ts`, `BchDtls`]. Three limits shape any design: the serial field is **one per invoice line**; the 1,000-line cap makes an IMEI-per-line policy a real ceiling; and **the date pattern the schema prints for expiry and warranty admits only the years 2010 to 2029** [W NIC], so a batch that expires in 2030 or later (a drug made in 2027 with 36 months, any five-year shelf life) does not fit the field as printed, and Comsky's own checker does not look at `ExpDt` at all (section 3.2). The whole hook matters only above the **₹5 crore** turnover line (still the figure on the compliance pages I read for 2026; a ₹2 crore step is described only as a proposal) [Ws EINV-THR], that is, to distributors and manufacturers, not to counters.
6. **IMEI is cheap to validate and awkward to store**: 15 digits, an 8-digit type code, a 6-digit serial and a Luhn check digit over the first 14 [W 3GPP]; a phone with two simultaneously active SIMs has two IMEIs [Ws GSMA]; two do not fit the 20-character e-invoice serial field. **Used-phone trade is the strongest reason for per-unit cost**: the second-hand margin scheme taxes selling price minus purchase price, item by item [W CBIC].
7. **Jewellery is serial-with-weights**: BIS assigns one six-character HUID per article [W BIS]; showing it on the invoice is voluntary today, and small shops and several classes of jewellery are exempt [W BIS-J]. **Variants with pooled cost (CF24) fit garments and do not fit jewellery or mobiles**, where cost differs per piece or per storage size. So the cost ladder is serial (per unit), batch (per lot), variant (pooled), and the model must say which an item uses.
8. **Label printing has two different customers.** Retailers print price, hang, shelf and scale labels (habit and convenience). Small manufacturers and packers print the **statutory pack label** (MRP including taxes, month and year of manufacture, net quantity, consumer care, batch, expiry, licence numbers) [W LM Rule 6, FSSAI, DR Rule 96]. The second is the law's. One label designer must serve both.
9. **Scanning is mostly "type a code and press Enter"** in a keyboard-first app; the design work is **resolution**: one code can be an item, a variant, a batch, a serial, a pack or a scale label, and a GS1 code carries several of these at once (GTIN, batch, expiry, serial) [Ws GS1-AI].
10. **Rank of Comsky customers each vertical may represent [J]**, by population x fit x Tally's gap (table in section 5): **1 pharma, 2 mobile and electronics, 3 FMCG and trading distribution, 4 garments and footwear, 5 manufacturers with batch genealogy, 6 auto parts, 7 grocery, 8 hardware and electricals, 9 agri inputs and food, 10 jewellery, 11 books.** The ordering is judgement with stated factors, not data.
11. **The minimal common model (section 6):** an item tracking mode (`none`, `batch`, `serial`) plus the variant fields CF24 already designs; a batch with number, dates, MRP, price, status; a serial with identifier(s), cost, warranty, status and a few attributes; a **barcode registry** mapping any code to an item, variant, batch, serial or pack; and a **label field catalogue** (about 24 fields). **Twelve presets built from those four mechanisms** (section 7); ship five first.
12. **A discrepancy I noticed, for the owner:** `docs/parity-data.json` rows `batches` and `expiry-reports` are marked `covered` but their `ours` text still says "No batch field on item or line" and "No batch/expiry concept", and the Batch & Expiry Summary screen is a `ComingSoon` stub [C parity-data.json:930-948, BatchSummary.tsx:4, catalogue.ts:205]. The project's own gotcha (a status and its note must agree) applies; I did not edit either. Re-checked on 2026-10-23: both rows are unchanged, and `mrp-pricing` (P3, missing) now sits beside a statutory retail-sale-price valuation (item 13).
13. **Two tax rules make the price on the pack a tracked fact, not just a number (new in this revision).** (i) For apparel and made-up textile articles the GST rate depends on the **sale value per piece**: 5% up to ₹2,500, 18% above; for footwear on the value **per pair**, with the same line; in force 22 Sep 2025 (the threshold was ₹1,000) [W GST56]. Comsky's rate engine has dated history and a per-line override but **no rule keyed on a line's unit value** [C `core/gstRates.ts`], which bites exactly where size or colour variants carry different prices. (ii) For pan masala, gutkha, cigarettes, unmanufactured and chewing tobacco the Council decided that GST is levied on the **retail sale price instead of the transaction value** [W GST56]; the valuation notification (19/2025-Central Tax, section 15(5)) took effect on **1 Feb 2026**, the highest of several printed prices is the RSP and a raised price counts [W RSP-NOTIF], and a companion Rule 31D deems the value to be the RSP less the tax in it [Ws RSP-NOTIF]. For those goods **the MRP on the pack is the tax base and must live on the batch**; Tally 7.1 (May 2026) added GST on a tax-inclusive MRP for tobacco goods [W TAL-REL]. (The comment at the top of `core/gstRates.ts` says it was unconfirmed whether the tobacco transition had taken effect; the notification above gives 1 Feb 2026.) (iii) A retail MRP includes the tax, so a line priced at MRP works backwards (taxable value = MRP / (1 + rate)); the voucher engine has no tax-inclusive line path except the advance receipt [R pos-pharma-market section 3.3].
14. **A month-only expiry has a stated meaning (new).** The 2025 amendment to the Cosmetics Rules 2020 defines "expiry date" as the last day of the labelled month and "use before" as before its first day [W COSM]; Drugs Rule 96 says the same of a month-and-year drug expiry (the explanation after sub-rule (4): the date of expiry is in terms of month and year, means the drug is recommended till the last day of the month, and is preceded by the words "Expiry date") [W DR 96]; FSSAI food dates are DD/MM/YY or, above three months of shelf life, month and year [W FSSAI 5(10)]. So entry accepts `MM/YY`, stores **the last day of the month**, and keeps which wording the label used, because "use before" ends a month earlier.

## 3. The regulatory spine: what the law makes a business record

| Instrument | What it forces | Fields it implies | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Drugs Rules 1945, Rule 65** (retail and wholesale licence conditions) | purchase records with date, supplier name, address and **licence number**, drug, quantity, **batch number**, manufacturer; wholesale sale memos with buyer licence number, drug, quantity, **batch number**, manufacturer and a signature, copies kept **three years**; a retail prescription register (or serially numbered credit memos for drugs in original containers) giving, for **Schedule C, H and H1**, the **manufacturer, batch number and expiry date**; a **separate Schedule H1 register** (prescriber, patient, drug, quantity; three years); other records at least two years; Schedule H, H1 and X on prescription only; **no sale or stocking after expiry**, except drugs already being withdrawn, reimbursed or disposed of, kept apart in cartons marked "Not for sale"; a drug supplied in a container other than the maker's goes only by a registered pharmacist with a label showing the drug, the quantity and the dealer's name and address | per line: batch, expiry, manufacturer; per party: licence number; per item: schedule; a batch **status** ("withdrawal pending, not for sale"); a printable **loose-dispense label** | [W DR 65(3), 65(3)(h), 65(4)(4), 65(5), 65(7), 65(9), 65(17), 65(19); a clause-by-clause table in the CDSCO copy's numbering is in R pos-pharma-market section 3.9] |
| **Drugs Rules, Rule 96** (labels) | the maker prints a distinctive batch number, the manufacturing and expiry dates (60 months at most unless extended), the manufacturing licence number | what a pharmacy scans is already on the pack | [W DR 96(1)(v), (vi), (vii)] |

### 3.1 Four things the law does not say, so the design must not assume
- **No source I read makes a retail pharmacy scan a code**, a retailer print an MRP, or a mobile shop record an IMEI. Recording the batch on the bill is law for Schedule H and H1 supplies [W DR 65(3)]; the rest is practice and brand policy; the 2022 QR rule binds the manufacturer, not the chemist [W CDSCO-FAQ].
- **No source says an invoice must show an IMEI, a HUID or an FSSAI number.** BIS says showing the HUID on an invoice is voluntary today and that keeping HUID records is not required [W BIS-J]; for an IMEI or an FSSAI number on an invoice I found nothing either way [unverified].
- **Expiry format:** FSSAI food dates are DD/MM/YY or, above three months of shelf life, month and year [W FSSAI 5(10)]; drug labels use month and year, which Rule 96 reads as good till the last day of that month, with the words "Expiry date" before it [W DR 96], and the 2025 cosmetics amendment says so in terms ("expiry date": last day of the labelled month; "use before": before its first day) [W COSM]. Entry must accept `MM/YY`, store the last day of the month and keep the wording [J].
- **Exchange-offer GST** is disputed across the consultant pages I found [Ws MARGIN]; a software default would be a tax opinion.

**Consequences [J].**
1. **Serial items:** the IMEI (15 characters) fits `PrdSlNo`; two IMEIs joined do not (31 with a separator), so the second goes to an attribute named IMEI2 or stays off the IRN. One IMEI per line means **one line per unit**, and the IRP's limit of 1,000 line items per invoice [W NIC; also W BUSY-IRN] makes that unworkable for a 1,200-handset dispatch. **Default: keep lines grouped by item and put the IMEI list on a printed annexure; offer one-line-per-unit only for a buyer that demands it and only below the cap.**
2. **Batch items:** one batch per line (as Comsky already does); a purchase of one item in three batches is three lines. A batch name under 3 characters is silently left out today [C einvoice.ts:358]; the batch capture screen should say so before the bill is posted.
3. **Who it touches:** only taxpayers above the **₹5 crore** aggregate-turnover mandate (notification 10/2023, from 1 Aug 2023; once crossed in any year since 2017-18 it stays; a separate 30-day reporting window applies from 1 Apr 2025 above ₹10 crore) [Ws EINV-THR]; compliance pages read in October 2026 still give ₹5 crore, with a ₹2 crore step described only as a proposal [Ws EINV-THR]. That is distributors, manufacturers and large chains; a counter shop never sends a `PrdSlNo`.
4. **Barcode** is the one field that costs nothing: send the item's primary code in `Barcde` when it is 3 to 30 characters [J].
5. **The expiry and warranty year limit (new).** The printed pattern for `ExpDt` and `WrDt` is the one used for the document date, which admits 2010 to 2029 [W NIC]; Comsky's own checker accepts 2000 to 2099 for the dates it checks and does not check `ExpDt` [C `einvoice.ts:589`, `:717-720`]. A pharma or food batch made from 2027 with a 36-month life, or any five-year shelf life, expires in 2030 or later. **Default [J]: omit `ExpDt` (and `WrDt`) when the year is above 2029, say so on the capture screen and in the e-invoice preview, and confirm against the IRP sandbox before relying on it**, rather than send a value the printed pattern rejects. Never invent a substitute date.

### 3.3 Identifier facts the model relies on
- **IMEI** [W 3GPP TS 23.003 6.2.1, Release 19]: 8-digit type allocation code, 6-digit serial, one check digit (spare, sent as zero, on the air); the check digit is the **Luhn** digit over the first 14 digits and exists to catch typing errors. Worked example (computed by me, not copied): the well-known sample 49-015420-323751 has check digit **8**, so `490154203237518` is valid and `490154203237581` (two digits swapped) is not. **Validation should warn, never block**: a transposition is a typing error, but a legitimately odd device must still be sellable [J].
- **Dual SIM:** each simultaneously active SIM has its own IMEI; a phone that can use only one SIM at a time may have one [Ws GSMA, partly my inference]. A handset can therefore carry one to three identifiers (two IMEIs, and for some makes a serial) [J].
- **EAN-13 and GTIN:** India's country prefix is 890, issued by GS1 India; the company prefix is longer; GTIN-14 identifies a case or pallet, not a single item [W GS1IN]. Check digit: weights 1 and 3 alternating over the first 12 digits (a different rule from Luhn: do not reuse one validator for both). Constructed example: `890123456789` gives `8901234567890`.
- **GS1 application identifiers** used on pharma and food cartons: 01 GTIN (14 digits), 10 batch or lot (up to 20), 17 expiry (`YYMMDD`), 21 serial (up to 20); the order is flexible, variable-length fields end with a group separator (ASCII 29), parentheses are print-only [Ws GS1-AI].
- **Variable-measure (weighed) codes:** GS1 reserves prefixes 20 to 29 for restricted circulation numbers; the item reference, the price or weight and their field lengths are defined by the retailer or the national member, not by one standard; a common layout is two prefix digits, a five-digit item code, a five-digit weight or price block and a check digit [Ws GS1-RCN]. Constructed example: prefix 21, item 00123, weight 01250 (1.250 kg) gives `2100123012503`. **The scale's configuration, not the ERP, decides the layout; the ERP must be told it.** **Indian POS documentation shows a different, non-EAN shape (new):** GoFrugal's RPOS6 article gives a 16-character code `W361802000004000` (a letter prefix W, a four-digit item code, five digits of weight in grams, six digits of price) and its RPOS7 guide gives `W00319801500` (W, a six-digit item code, five digits of grams) and `U00319700001` for a counted item (U, a six-digit item code, a five-digit quantity); it says the format may vary by scale maker, and the shop exports its item list as a CSV or XML file for the scale vendor to load [W GOFR-SCALE]. So a profile must accept a letter prefix, any field lengths, and a count as well as a weight or a price.
- **ISBN-13** is an EAN-13 with prefix 978 or 979 ("Bookland") [Ws ISBN]; India's agency issues them free to Indian publishers through isbn.gov.in [Ws ISBN]. Constructed check: `978938123456` gives `9789381234563`.

## 4. The verticals

Each entry has the same shape: the real-world process, the unit of tracking, what is tracked, the scan flows, the labels (what, who prints), the reports, the regulatory hooks, the pain with Tally, what breaks a naive model, the model that fits, and a weight. **Process descriptions marked [J] are general trade knowledge I could not source; they are the first thing to confirm with five shops per vertical (section 8).** Label sizes are collected in section 5.6 from marketplace listings and vendor pages [Ws]; no label was printed and none was checked against a printer.

### (a) Mobile phones and consumer electronics

**Process.** Brand, then national or regional distributor, then retailer (large chains buy direct) [J]. The brand's sale invoice to the distributor comes with the units' IMEIs [J; consistent with a brand DOA policy that asks for the original invoice **with IMEI details**, the box and accessories, and runs the window from activation or proof of purchase, whichever is first [Ws DOA]]. The retailer sells handsets (serial), accessories (barcoded, not serial), insurance and extended warranty, and takes **exchanges, buybacks and used phones**; EMI finance is common [W BUSY-MOB]. Brand schemes and **price-drop credit** exist in the channel: trade-press summaries of September 2026 say brands and channel partners are moving to targeted support, namely margin support, **price-protection guarantees** and sell-through incentives linked to retail movement [Ws BS-MOB], and one brand's consumer policy refunds a price cut within 14 days for up to ten units [Ws APPLE-PP]; I found **nothing public on how a distributor or retailer claims price protection on units in stock**, so its mechanics (an IMEI list of units held on the drop date) remain [J].
**Items and volume [J].** A counter carries tens to a few hundred live handset models (each storage size its own item) and a thousand or so accessory items; the number that matters is units: a counter sells tens to hundreds of IMEIs a month and a distributor tens of thousands a year (section 6.8 prices 180,000 units a year as the design scale).
**Unit of tracking: serial (the IMEI).** Model plus RAM and storage are separate items because cost and price differ [J]; colour is an attribute **of the unit** (a given IMEI is one colour), not a stock dimension [J]. Accessories: plain items with a barcode.
**Tracked per unit:** IMEI 1, IMEI 2, serial where the brand has one, brand, model, storage, colour, **MRP printed on the box**, purchase price, purchase voucher, sale price, sale voucher and date, customer, warranty months by brand or model and whether it starts at invoice or activation [W ACC-MOB; Ws DOA], exchange value, status (in stock, sold, returned, DOA, in service, replaced, written off), replacement IMEI.
**Scan flows.** *Inward:* scan each box's IMEI barcode against the brand's list, so a missing or extra unit shows before the bill is posted [J]. *Sale:* scan the box or type the IMEI; the unit must be in stock and unsold; model, price and warranty fill [W LOGIC]. *Return or DOA:* scan the IMEI, find the original sale, restock or send to claim; a replacement unit has a new IMEI and the **old warranty end date** [J]. *Stock take:* scan every unit, compare with the book. *Transfer between shops:* scan out and scan in. *Used phone in:* type the IMEI (Luhn warns), record the unit's purchase price, a "blacklist checked" flag (no API found).
**Labels.** The maker prints the box label with the IMEIs and barcodes [J]. The retailer prints shelf price cards, accessory EAN labels, a stock label on used and refurbished units (IMEI, grade, price, own code), warranty stickers and repair-job tags [J].
**Reports.** Serial-wise stock with age; IMEI lookup (purchase, sale, warranty, returns in one view); model and storage stock; dead stock; per-unit profit and brand profit [W ACC-MOB]; warranty expiring; DOA and returns register; **a claim sheet: the IMEI list in stock on a given date** (for a price-drop claim) [J]; the used-device margin statement.
**Regulatory hooks.** `PrdSlNo`, `OrgCntry`, `WrDt` (not beyond 2029), `Barcde` (section 3.2); MRP on the box [W LM 6]; IMEI registration and tampering rules [Ws DOT]; margin scheme [W CBIC].
**Pain with Tally.** No serial; the IMEI-as-batch trick means one line per unit in the voucher and "closing quantity 1" as the only unsold test [Ws TAL-IMEI]; no warranty, no IMEI lookup, no second IMEI, no per-unit cost for the margin scheme; the only barcode is the item alias [W TAL-BAR]; an add-on from 2017 imports serials from Excel [Ws]; Tally's own GST guide for mobile retailers does not mention IMEI, batches or warranty [W TAL-GST-MOB].
**What breaks a naive model.** Two IMEIs per handset; a DOA replacement with a new IMEI and the old warranty end; a return of a unit sold to a different customer than the one returning it; an IMEI keyed with a slip (Luhn catches every single-digit error and almost every adjacent swap, but not a swap of 09 and 90 [R batch-serial-tracking section 3.1.1]); leading zeros in an IMEI imported through Excel as a number; a free earphone bundled with a phone; a price drop on units already in stock; the 1,000-line e-invoice cap; used phones bought from an unregistered person without an IMEI record; a unit moved between two of the owner's godowns mid-sale.
**Model that fits.** `serial` mode (kind IMEI, second serial on, warranty), unit cost and MRP on the unit, **specific-unit costing** for the margin scheme, no tracked variants, models as separate items, a scheme as a price level, accessories as plain barcoded items. **Weight: high (rank 2).**

### (b) Pharma wholesale and retail

**Process.** Maker, then stockist or distributor, then retail chemist or hospital pharmacy [J]. A chemist sells prescription and over-the-counter medicines, **loose strips**, and surgical and general goods on one bill; a stockist sells to chemists, with **schemes** (free quantity or discount) and **expiry returns** to the company [J]. The law fixes the records (section 3): purchase records, wholesale memos, the prescription register or serially numbered credit memos, the H1 register, the ban on sale after expiry, and the loose-dispense label [W DR 65].
**Items and volume.** A chemist's catalogue runs to thousands of items; one stockist's notes put a neighbourhood store near 30,000 SKUs [Ws SKU-PH, a single anecdote]. A stockist holds a few live batches per item, and a 50-line purchase entry is the daily unit of work [J].
**Unit of tracking: batch**, identified by item, batch number and **MRP**, carrying expiry. Below the batch, unit conversion: box, strip, tablet [W BUSY-MED lists strip, pack, piece, box].
**Tracked.** Item (brand), generic or **salt**, manufacturer, pack size, **schedule** (H, H1, X, NDPS, over-the-counter), HSN and GST rate, drug licence number of the party; per batch: number, manufacturing date, expiry (month and year), **MRP**, purchase rate, **PTR and PTS** (PTR is defined as the price at which the maker sells to a retailer or hospital, and the price regulator averages PTR into ceiling prices [Ws PTR]; PTS appears in a parliamentary panel's finding on the gap between the price to the stockist and the MRP [Ws PTR]; the usual margins between PTS, PTR and MRP are trade usage I could not source [J]), scheme, rack, cold-chain flag [J]. Per H/H1 supply: prescriber name and address, patient name and address, serial number, pharmacist's signature [W DR 65(3)].
**Scan flows.** *Inward:* scanning the strip or box code (a QR or barcode with batch, manufacturing and expiry on the top-300 brands, its layout set by each maker [W CDSCO-FAQ]) would fill item, batch and expiry, which is the slowest part of a pharma purchase entry [J]. *Sale:* scan the strip; the batch is **suggested earliest-expiry-first with stock** [W ACC-PH describes it]; H/H1 lines prompt for prescriber and patient. *Expiry return:* pick the batches to return against the supplier. *Quarantine:* mark a batch "withdrawal pending, not for sale" [W DR 65(17)]. *Stock take:* scan.
**Labels.** The maker prints every pack label (Rule 96) [W DR 96]. **The chemist prints the loose-dispense label** (drug, quantity, dealer name and address) [W DR 65(19)] and rack or shelf labels [J]. The stockist prints dispatch and cold-chain carton labels [J].
**Reports.** Batch-wise stock; expiring in 30, 60 and 90 days [W ACC-PH]; expired and return-pending register; per-supplier short-expiry return list; H/H1 register with the fields of Rule 65(3) (printable as the statutory register); the bill itself as the Schedule H/H1 record (it must carry manufacturer, batch and expiry on those lines); purchase register with supplier licence number; margin by batch; scheme and claim statements [J].
**Regulatory hooks.** `BchDtls.Nm` (3 to 20) and `ExpDt` **(not beyond 2029, section 3.2)** for stockists above ₹5 crore; HSN; MRP is not an e-invoice field; the QR rule binds the maker and fixes content, not encoding [W CDSCO-FAQ]; **ceiling prices** for scheduled formulations, with the dealer's duty to show the maker's price list, change each April [R pos-pharma-market section 3.7]; the GST routes for returning time-expired drugs (a fresh invoice, or a delivery challan and a credit note) [R pos-pharma-market section 3.5]; Rule 65 retention periods (two years generally, three for wholesale memo copies and the H1 register) [W DR 65(5)(2), 65(3)(h), 65(7)].
**Pain with Tally.** Batch name, manufacturing date and expiry exist; **no per-batch MRP** in the pages I read; the sale batch is chosen by hand with no earliest-expiry suggestion claimed [W TAL-BATCH]; no schedule flag or H1 register in anything I read; Tally's own help calls batches mainly a pharmaceutical requirement [Ws TAL-HELP].
**What breaks a naive model.** The same batch number from two makers; the same batch at two MRPs after a price revision [W LM 18(3)] (the September 2025 rate cuts made this real: revised MRPs from 22 Sep 2025, with no duty to re-sticker old stock if the price benefit reaches the buyer [R pos-pharma-market section 3.3]); loose-strip conversion (an open strip has no purchase invoice and loses its batch and expiry when cut; the stock report shows whole strips plus loose units per batch [R pos-pharma-market section 3.2]); **free goods from a different batch than the billed goods**; a purchase bill not yet entered, so the sale drives the batch negative; a return whose batch was never bought here; physician samples that must never be stocked for sale [W DR 65(18)]; expiry typed `MM/YY`; a batch spread across racks and godowns.
**Model that fits.** `batch` mode with MRP identity, price levels at batch level for PTR and PTS, a `schedule` flag on the item, licence number on the party, a batch **status** (active, hold, expired, blocked), earliest-expiry suggestion on by default, pack hierarchy by the existing compound units (a price per strip, a derived per-unit rate), an H1 register built from the voucher log. The pharmacy sibling lists ten requirements of the batch design, R1 to R10 [R pos-pharma-market section 6.4]; the model in section 6 covers them, with one open difference (the identity paragraph of 6.2). **Two presets: retail and wholesale. Weight: very high (rank 1).**

### (c) FMCG and general-trading distribution

**Process.** A distributor or sub-distributor buys from the company, sells to retailers through salesmen and vans on beats, carries company **schemes** and claims them back, takes returns for expiry and damage, and deals with MRP changes [J]; distributor-management vendors describe returns logged by reason (expired, damaged, wrong item) that open a claim, and scheme credits settled by credit note against slabs [Ws DMS]. The federation claims over four lakh distributors [Ws MKT]. Tally's own guide: a van is a godown, a Stock Journal loads it, a load sheet is signed, shortfall is flagged; schemes sit in price levels [W TAL-FMCG].
**Items and volume [J].** One company's range at a distributor is a few hundred SKUs and a multi-company distributor carries a few thousand; a purchase invoice from a company runs to dozens or hundreds of lines, most of them single-batch.
**Unit of tracking: item, with batch switched on for items that expire** (foods, dairy, personal care) and **MRP per batch**; barcodes: the consumer unit's EAN and the **case's 14-digit GTIN** with a pack quantity [W GS1IN].
**Tracked.** Item, EAN, MRP, pack and case quantity, trade price, scheme (free goods, slab discount), batch and expiry where relevant, beat and salesman (custom fields), van.
**Scan flows.** *Inward:* scan the case code, which adds the pack quantity; *van load:* scan; *delivery:* the salesman's invoice; *returns:* scan batch and expiry; *stock take:* scan.
**Labels.** Rare. Dispatch, van and bin labels; the distributor must **not** obliterate or re-stamp an MRP [W LM 18(5)].
**Reports.** Batch-wise stock with expiry, **stock by MRP**, scheme-wise sales, van reconciliation (Tally has it), near-expiry and damage returns, company claim statements [J].
**Regulatory hooks.** MRP and its revision rule [W LM 18]; **for pan masala and tobacco goods the taxable value is the declared RSP less the tax, from 1 Feb 2026, the highest printed price applying** [W RSP-NOTIF], so the batch MRP is a tax input there; FSSAI for food [W FSSAI]; e-invoice for the larger distributors.
**Pain with Tally.** The guide mentions neither batch nor MRP [W TAL-FMCG]; **the same item at two MRPs is, in practice, two items** [J]; schemes by price level only; the one MRP feature in the 2026 releases is GST on a tax-inclusive MRP for tobacco goods [W TAL-REL].
**What breaks.** Two MRPs in stock; a scheme's free goods; returns at the old MRP; case-to-piece conversion; van spoilage.
**Model that fits.** Optional `batch` with MRP, a barcode registry with pack quantity, price levels for schemes. **Weight: high (rank 3).**

### (d) Garments and footwear

**Process.** Makers and wholesalers (Surat, Tirupur and similar) sell by design number and size run; retailers sell by variant; seasons drive markdowns [J]. Branded retail uses brand tags; traders print their own [J].
**Items and volume.** One retailer example is **350 styles x 24 size and colour variants, about 8,000 live SKUs** [W GING]; a wholesaler's catalogue is design-led and size runs are bought in sets [J].
**Unit of tracking: variant** (style x size x colour), **which is what CF24 designs**: up to three tracking fields per item, (item, variant) a stock position, **cost pooled per item** [C .wt-cf AD.3]. Pooled cost suits garments, where cost is mostly per style [J]. **Batches here mean "arrival date or season" for ageing** [W GOFR, VYAP], which is derivable from purchase vouchers and should not be modelled as a batch [J].
**Tracked.** Style or design number, brand, season, fabric, fit, size, colour, MRP (can differ by size [J]), cost, one barcode per variant, arrival date.
**Scan flows.** *Inward:* label per piece at receipt (quantity = pieces received) [J]; CF24 does **not** build a size-run grid, each size is a line [C .wt-cf AD.3.6]. *Sale:* scan the tag. *Exchange:* scan the old tag, then the new. *Stock take and transfer:* scan.
**Labels.** Hang tag or sticker: brand, style, size, colour, MRP, barcode, design number, and often a private **cost code** [J]. Printed per piece at receipt by the retailer or wholesaler for unbranded goods; pre-printed by the brand otherwise [J]. Law: a garment sold **loose** is outside the pack declarations when the garment itself carries the maker or importer's name and address, country of origin, consumer-care phone and email, the size (S, M, L with cm or m) and the MRP inclusive of all taxes [W LM-GARM]; a **pre-packed** garment follows Rule 6 [W LM 6(1)]. **The GST rate depends on the sale value per piece** (5% up to ₹2,500, 18% above; footwear the same per pair) [W GST56], so a variant's price is a tax input (section 3).
**Reports.** Size-by-colour stock matrix; sell-through by style, size, colour; aged stock; markdown candidates; label print list.
**Pain with Tally.** Item per size or colour in the name [R]; one 32-character alias per item [W TAL-BAR]; no label designer. Scale of the problem: 350 styles x 24 variants is about 8,000 live SKUs [W GING].
**What breaks.** Barcodes reused across seasons; MRP per size; a size exchange; an item explosion if each variant is an item; a size or colour priced either side of ₹2,500, which changes the GST rate.
**Model that fits.** CF24 tracking fields (Colour, Size, one more), **a barcode per variant resolving to (item, variant key)**, optional MRP and rate override per variant (**a new slice**: addendum AD.5 row 25 and AD.6 list per-variant price, barcode and SKU as a different feature, not part of CF-9), a **GST-rate-by-unit-value rule** (decision 11), hang-tag label. **Weight: medium-high (rank 4).**

### (e) Hardware, electricals, sanitary and appliances

**Process [J].** Many SKUs by size, rating and brand; the brand's **list price with a discount** more than the printed MRP in many lines; units in pieces, kilograms, metres; cable sold from **coils or drums** with a remaining length; appliances (fans, geysers, pumps, inverters, batteries, air-conditioners) carry a serial or date code with a warranty; paints carry a shade and batch.
**Items and volume [J].** A dealer lists thousands of items (an accounting firm's guide says "thousands of SKUs" from screws to power tools [Ws SKU-HW]); appliance units are few and valuable, and the long tail is untracked.
**Unit.** Item by default; **serial for appliances**; **batch for paint, adhesives and chemicals**; a drum or coil is a batch whose quantity (metres) drains; **tiles** carry a shade and a caliber code beside the lot, and one job should be served from one lot [Ws TILE], so a batch needs a few free attributes.
**Scan flows.** Mostly inward checking and a counter lookup; many goods are unlabelled, so the shop labels them.
**Labels.** The shop prints rack, bin and price labels for loose items [J]; boxes carry the maker's label.
**Reports.** Stock by size and brand; price-list comparison; warranty by serial; slow movers; drum or coil balance.
**Pain with Tally.** Moderate. Price lists and units exist; the gap is serial and warranty for appliances and barcodes [W TAL-BAR].
**What breaks.** A cable cut from a drum; mixed units on one line; a list-price revision.
**Model that fits.** Items plus price levels; optional serial per item (with the maker's date code as a unit attribute); batch for paint, tiles (shade and caliber as batch attributes) and drums. **Weight: medium (rank 8).**

### (f) Grocery and supermarket

**Process.** Packaged goods with an EAN; loose goods weighed at a counter (rice, pulses, spices, dry fruit); goods packed in store; dairy and bakery with short expiry; shelf labels reprinted when a price changes [J]. A weighing instrument used in trade must be verified and stamped and re-verified periodically, on a schedule that is state-specific [Ws LM-WM].
**Items and volume.** A kirana store trades around 4,200 SKUs on one trade source and a supermarket chain is reported above 28,000 [Ws SKU-GROC, two single sources]; a counter handles thousands of bills a day, so scan and print speed is the load, not the item count [J].
**Unit.** Item by EAN; a **PLU** for loose goods; batch optional for short-expiry lines (earliest-expiry shelf rotation).
**Tracked.** EAN, MRP, selling price (often below MRP), PLU, tare, unit, expiry for some lines, packed-on date for in-store packs.
**Scan flows.** POS scanning of an EAN or a **scale label** (a 13-digit code with prefix 20 to 29 carrying the item code and the weight or the price in a layout the scale defines [Ws GS1-RCN], or, in an Indian POS vendor's documents, a letter-prefixed code such as W plus item plus grams, and U plus item plus count [W GOFR-SCALE]); inward by EAN; handheld stock take; shelf-label reprint.
**Labels.** *Shelf-edge labels* (the per-unit price rule applies where the unit price differs from the pack price [W LM 6(11)]); *scale labels* (printed by the scale; the ERP sends the item-code and price file and must **parse** the label); *in-store pack labels* (packed-on date, MRP, net quantity, and for food the FSSAI particulars; anyone who pre-packs carries the declarations [W LM 4(1)]; a scoop weighed in front of the buyer is not a pre-packaged commodity, because the Act's definition turns on the buyer not being present, while a pack made in advance is [W LM-ACT]).
**Reports.** Item sales by hour and by counter; shrinkage; near-expiry; label reprint list.
**Pain with Tally.** POS is thin; scanning is the alias; no scale integration; no PLU or weighed-code parsing [W TAL-BAR].
**What breaks.** A scale's layout differing from the POS profile (a Hobart-style scale printing a code the POS reads as an item with no price [Ws GS1-RCN]); a PLU collision; returns of weighed goods; two prices for one EAN; a new item or a rate change that is not re-sent to the scale as a file [W GOFR-SCALE].
**Model that fits.** Barcode registry kind `scale` with a layout profile (prefix, item digits, value digits, weight or price, decimals), `weighed` item flag with PLU, optional batch. Mostly served by POS products, so **weight: medium (rank 7)**.

### (g) Jewellery

**Process.** A counter sells **tagged pieces**: each tag carries the item or design code, gross, stone and net weight, purity (22K916 style), and a price built from net weight x the day's rate plus making or wastage plus stone [Ws JEW; W BUSY-JEW]. Old gold is taken in with a melting-loss deduction and purity adjustment [W BUSY-JEW]. Metal and jewellery go out to and come back from **karigars** (job work) [W TAL-JEW]. Pieces go to a BIS assaying and hallmarking centre, which **laser-marks the BIS logo, the purity and a six-character HUID on each article**, a different HUID per article, from a code issued by BIS's server; the jeweller must be BIS-registered [W BIS]. Registered jewellers numbered 1,94,039 in November 2024 [Ws MKT].
**Items and volume [J].** A showroom holds hundreds to tens of thousands of tagged pieces; each piece is one record, so the unit count is the item count and a tag is printed once per piece.
**Unit of tracking: the tag, a serial with weights.** Loose metal and stones are tracked in bulk by weight and purity [J]. Cost is **per piece**, so CF24's pooled variant cost does **not** fit [C .wt-cf AD.3.1 says so for "a jeweller's 22 K against 18 K"].
**Tracked per tag.** Tag number, design or item code, metal and purity, gross, stone and net weight (three decimals [J]), making and wastage, stone charges, **HUID**, hallmark centre and date, purchase or job-work cost, status (in stock, sold, with karigar, at hallmark, on approval), barcode or QR.
**Scan flows.** *Counter:* scan the tag; weights and the live rate price the line. *Inward from a karigar or wholesaler:* enter the piece, print the tag. *Hallmark:* record each piece's HUID on return (reading the laser mark is manual unless the centre supplies a list [unverified]). *Stock take:* scan tags against the book; this trade audits often [J].
**Labels.** The **centre** marks the HUID [W BIS]. The **jeweller** prints the tag: code, weights, purity, price or a price code, HUID, barcode or QR [J]. Tag shapes (a dumbbell or rat-tail tag) and sizes are in section 5.6, from non-Indian listings; I found no Indian jewellery-tag source.
**Reports.** Tag-wise stock; weight-wise stock by purity; karigar balance; sales by design; HUID register; old-gold purchase register; daily rate sheet [J].
**Regulatory hooks.** Hallmarking order and HUID [W BIS]; **exempt:** turnover up to ₹40 lakh, articles under 2 grams, kundan, polki and jadau, districts outside the order; **the HUID on the invoice and HUID records are voluntary today** [W BIS-J]; silver HUID from 1 Sep 2025 [Ws HUID]; HSN and rates (vendor pages claim 3% on the metal and other rates on charges; **verify against the notification** [Ws JEW]); `Barcde` and `AttribDtls` could carry the tag and the HUID [J].
**Pain with Tally.** Per-piece weight, purity and tag number are claimed, **HUID, hallmark and barcode are not mentioned** [W TAL-JEW]; computed amounts need a formula field [R tally-udf-and-competitors §4].
**What breaks.** Re-tagging after repair or resizing; a piece split into two or made from old gold; a re-hallmarked piece carrying a new HUID [unverified]; a set (necklace plus earrings) on one tag, although **each article has its own HUID** (so a tag holds a list of HUIDs); daily rate changes; weights to three decimals.
**Model that fits.** `serial` mode with kind `tag`, typed weight and purity attributes, HUID as a second identifier, **specific-unit costing**, a tag label. **Weight: low in count, high in value (rank 10); specialised competitors exist [J].**

### (h) Automobile parts

**Process [J unless tagged].** Stockists and counters; **the part number is the key**, and one part has an OEM number, several aftermarket numbers, private-label codes, a supersession chain and a fitment list (make, model, year, engine) [Ws AUTO]. Tyres carry a four-digit week-and-year date code, though not every Indian make prints it in the DOT form [Ws TYRE]; batteries carry a manufacture-date sticker whose coding differs by brand and an in-service sticker that starts the warranty [Ws BATT]; lubricants carry a batch [J].
**Items and volume.** Thousands of part numbers, and one part can carry dozens of identifying numbers (OEM and aftermarket brands) on one vendor's account [Ws SKU-AUTO]; fast movers are a small fraction of the range [J].
**Unit.** Item with **many codes**; serial for batteries and tyres; batch for lubricants and coolants. Comsky has one `alias` and one `partNo` per item (`types.ts`), so a many-to-one code set is missing [C].
**Scan flows.** Counter lookup by **any** of an item's codes; inward by the maker's box code; the shop's own rack labels.
**Labels.** Bin and rack labels with part number and location; price tags for accessories [J].
**Reports.** Cross-reference lookup, fast and slow movers, supplier price lists, warranty claims by serial.
**Regulatory hooks.** MRP and HSN; I fetched nothing specific to parts [unverified]. Tally's automobile page speaks of part codes and barcodes and **does not show cross-reference or supersession** [Ws AUTO].
**What breaks.** A superseded number (stock sits under the old code); the same part sold under three brand numbers; kits and cores.
**Model that fits.** The **barcode registry doubles as an alias registry** (kind `alias`, many codes to one item); serial optional per item. **Weight: medium-low (rank 6).**

### (i) Books

**Process [J].** Title and edition are the item; the **ISBN-13 is the barcode** [Ws ISBN]; the publisher prints the price; trade discount by publisher; **sale or return** consignment is common. No batch, no serial.
**Items and volume [J].** A bookshop holds thousands to tens of thousands of titles, each a single item with its own ISBN; stock is counted by title.
**Scan flows.** POS and inward by ISBN; stock take.
**Labels.** Rare: a shop sticker for location or a changed price.
**Regulatory hooks.** GST on printed books: the pages I found disagree (nil on one, 5% on another for the same code, with a 2025 restructuring noted), so **no rate is stated here; check the schedule** [Ws ISBN]. Whether Legal Metrology's pack rules bind books is [unverified].
**Pain with Tally.** The alias holds a 13-digit ISBN well [W TAL-BAR]; consignment and sale-or-return are the gap [J].
**Model that fits.** A plain item with its code in the barcode registry. No new mechanism. **Weight: low (rank 11).**

### (j) Manufacturing with lot genealogy (raw-material lot to finished lot)

**Process.** Raw material arrives with the supplier's lot; a production batch gets an internal number and **consumes specific lots in specific quantities**; finished lots go to customers. Law and practice make the batch the **key into the manufacturing record**: the Drugs Rules define a batch by the manufacturing details recorded for it and available for inspection [W DR 96(1)(v)]; the Insecticides Rules have each batch analysed and the result recorded before it is packed [W INS 17]; FSSAI defines a lot or batch number as the mark by which food can be traced in manufacture and identified in distribution [W FSSAI 2(l)]; the revised Schedule M (GSR 922(E), Jan 2024; small and medium makers by 31 Dec 2025) lists batch processing and packaging records among its documents [Ws SCHM], and the Cosmetics Rules 2020 put the batch number and dates on the label [Ws COSM-L]; I did not read the retention periods.
**Items and volume [J].** A small manufacturer has tens of raw materials and tens of finished goods but hundreds to thousands of **lots** a year, so the lot count, not the item count, sizes the genealogy table.
**Unit: batch**, on raw and finished items, joined by a **link** (finished batch <- component batch, quantity).
**Tracked.** Bill of materials, planned and actual consumption, yield and loss, by-products, rejects, QC status, manufacturing date, expiry (from shelf life), batch cost (materials plus conversion), the output's MRP.
**Scan flows.** *Issue to production:* scan the raw lot label. *Finished goods:* print batch and carton labels. *Dispatch:* scan.
**Labels.** **The manufacturer prints the statutory pack label**: MRP including taxes, month and year, net quantity, consumer care, batch, expiry, and the licence numbers (FSSAI or manufacturing licence) [W LM 6, FSSAI 5, DR 96]. This is the label-printing customer the law creates.
**Reports.** Forward and backward trace ("raw lot X went into which finished lots, and which customers got them"), the recall list, batch cost sheet, yield variance, expiry, consumption by lot.
**Pain with Tally.** It names the question but not a feature [W TAL-MFG]; Comsky's Manufacturing Journal also captures **no batch or expiry** today [C parity-data.json:3839].
**What breaks.** Partial lot consumption; blending two lots; rework or repack making a new batch that keeps the old expiry; split and merge; co-products.
**Model that fits.** `batch` mode plus a `batchLink` fact on a manufacturing voucher line (component batch, quantity, finished batch). CF24 says the Manufacturing Journal books **Not set** for variants in v1; batch genealogy is a separate change to the same voucher [C .wt-cf AD.3.4]. **Weight: medium (rank 5).**

### (k) Agri and food

**Food.** Processors, packers and traders of staples (flour, rice, spices, oil, dry fruit) pack in-house and sell loose; the pack label must carry lot, packing date, expiry or best-before and the FSSAI licence number [W FSSAI 5(7) to 5(10)]; the premises display the licence or registration [W FSSAI 5(7)(d)].
**Agri inputs (pesticide dealers).** The label carries batch number and expiry date; sales go in a register by product; officials can ask for every buyer of a batch; **date-expired stock must be segregated, stamped not for sale and kept in a marked place** [W INS]. **Fertilisers:** the Control Order bars a sale above the fixed maximum price, makes a retail dealer display each fertiliser's **opening stock for the day** and the price list, requires a cash or credit memo in Form M and lets the Controller direct records in Form N (clauses 3, 4, 5, 35) [W FCO]. **Seeds:** the label and the certification tag carry the net content, the date of testing, the **lot number** and a validity date; no seed is sold after it, and a seller keeps a **complete record of each lot sold for three years** (rules 8 and 13) [W SEEDS]. What Forms M and N contain and the Act's own list of label particulars I did not read.
**Commodity lots (grain, pulses).** A lot is a physical consignment with its own grade, moisture and rate, sold by weight [J]: a batch with typed attributes.
**Items and volume [J].** A packer or agri-inputs dealer carries hundreds of items; a packer makes a handful of batches a day.
**Unit: batch** with expiry; for commodities a lot with grade attributes.
**Labels.** Packers print pack labels (as in (j)); dealers print shelf labels [J].
**Reports.** Batch stock with expiry, expired and quarantined register, sales register by product with licence numbers, lot-wise stock by grade [J].
**Pain with Tally.** Batch with expiry exists; no licence, quarantine or sales-register print, and no pack label.
**Model that fits.** `batch` mode with `status`, party licence number, a register print; the packer's label as in (j). **Weight: low-medium (rank 9).**

## 5. Cross-vertical synthesis

### 5.1 The unit of tracking, by vertical

| Vertical | Serial | Batch | Variant (CF24) | Pack code | Weighed | Cost granularity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Mobile, electronics | **the IMEI or serial** | for accessories with expiry only | model and storage as items; colour on the unit | box | no | **per unit** |
| (b) Pharma | no | **batch + MRP** | no | box, strip | no (loose by tablet) | item method |
| (c) FMCG distribution | no | optional, MRP per batch | no | **case GTIN** | no | item method |
| (d) Garments, footwear | no | arrival date only | **style, size, colour** | per piece | no | pooled per item |
| (e) Hardware, electricals | appliances | paint, drums | rarely | box | by metre or kg | item method |
| (f) Grocery | no | optional (short expiry) | no | EAN | **scale code** | item method |
| (g) Jewellery | **the tag** | no | no | no | bulk metal | **per piece** |
| (h) Auto parts | batteries, tyres | lubricants | no | many aliases | no | item method |
| (i) Books | no | no | no | ISBN | no | item method |
| (j) Manufacturing | no | **raw and finished + link** | no | carton | by weight | batch cost |
| (k) Agri, food | no | **batch + expiry** | no | pack | by weight | item method |

### 5.2 Five questions that choose the unit [J]
1. Does a buyer, a brand or a regulator ask **"which unit" or "which lot"**? (Warranty, DOA, recall, Schedule H: yes; apparel: no.)
2. Does **price or cost differ** per unit or per lot? (MRP revision: per batch; used phone, jewel: per unit; garments: per style.)
3. Does the thing arrive with **its own identifier** from the maker (IMEI, HUID, GTIN with batch)? Then capture it, never invent one.
4. How many **units per line**? (Hundreds of IMEIs on one distributor invoice versus one tag on a counter bill.)
5. **Who prints the label**: the maker (scan it), the retailer (design it), or the law (a statutory panel)?

### 5.3 The cost ladder and what it means for the engine [J]
- **Variant (CF24):** pooled item cost, shared by quantity; exact quantity, allocated value [C .wt-cf AD.3.5]. Right for garments.
- **Batch:** the item's costing method stays; a batch carries its purchase rate for information and for batch-wise margin. (Tally lets batches differ by cost [W TAL-BATCH]; whether to value by batch is a separate decision.)
- **Serial or tag:** a **specific-unit cost** (the unit's own purchase cost) is the only honest answer for the margin scheme and for jewellery. The engine's costing is per item today [C inventory.ts, valuation.ts]; a per-unit method is a new costing mode and the biggest engine decision in this work (section 8, decision 1). It must reconcile to the item's closing value to the paisa, as CF24's allocation does.

### 5.4 What is scanned, who put the code there, what the system does

| The code is | Put there by | On scan the system must |
|---|---|---|
| an item EAN or GTIN | the maker (GS1 India, prefix 890) | resolve to the item; add `packQty` for a case; warn on an unknown code |
| a GS1 code with batch, expiry, serial (AI 01, 10, 17, 21) | the maker (food cartons; the pharma top-300 QR fixes its content, not its encoding) | parse and fill item, batch, expiry, serial; verify the expiry is not past |
| an IMEI (15 digits) | the maker | Luhn-check (warn), find the unit, refuse a sale of an unsold-already or not-in-stock unit |
| a variant barcode | the retailer or the brand | resolve to (item, variant key); keep resolving after an option is renamed (ids are stable, CF4) |
| a batch or lot label | the packer or the retailer | resolve to (item, batch) |
| a scale label (an EAN-13 with prefix 20 to 29, or a letter prefix such as W or U in Indian POS documents) | the scale | parse by the configured profile: item code plus weight, price or count |
| a tag or QR | the jeweller or refurbisher | resolve to the unit |
| an alias or part number | the shop | resolve to the item (existing behaviour) |

**Hardware assumption.** A keyboard-wedge scanner types the digits and an Enter, which Tally's own page confirms in spirit (scanner software installed, scanner connected) [W TAL-BAR]; in the key stack a scan is typed text, so it lands in the focused field and ends with Enter. Camera scanning belongs to the mobile track and is not designed here [J].

### 5.5 Ranking of Comsky customers by vertical [J]

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pharma (retail and wholesale) | 5 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 100 | huge population, law forces batch and expiry on the bill, Tally has no MRP-per-batch or H1 register |
| 2 | Mobile and consumer electronics | 4 | 4 | 5 | 3 | 80 | the owner's own example; Tally has no serial; used-phone margin needs per-unit cost |

## 6. The minimal common tracking model

**A proposal [J] for the design document to adopt or amend; it is not a specification.** Every field is optional and additive (the superset rule: a company that sets nothing sees today's app, asserted by identity); every name below is a working name. It serves all eleven verticals with **one item switch, three record shapes, one registry and one label catalogue**, and it **reuses CF24's variant fields instead of inventing a second variant mechanism** [C .wt-cf AD.3].

### 6.1 Item tracking mode and flags (on the stock item)

| Field | Values | Why (vertical) |
|---|---|---|
| `tracking` | `none` (default), `batch`, `serial`. Today's `batches: true` reads as `batch`, so every saved item stays valid | three behaviours, not one: Busy keeps separate switches for batch, serial and parameters [W BUSY-FAQ44]; Tally has batch only [W TAL-BATCH] |
| `mrpAt` | none, `item`, `batch`, `unit` | MRP is on the pack by law [W LM 6]; batch for pharma, FMCG, food; item for electronics, garments, books; **where the MRP is also the tax base (pan masala, tobacco) the batch MRP feeds the value** (decision 12) |
| `shelfLifeDays` | number | expiry is computed from manufacturing date or the voucher date when only a start is typed; Busy offers "expiry days" [W BUSY-BAT] |
| `expiryPolicy` | warning days list (90, 60, 30); sale of an expired batch: `allow`, `warn` (default), `block` | Rule 65(17) bars sale after expiry [W DR]; Busy has a warning period and an action for expired batches [W BUSY-BAT] |
| `pick` | `manual` (today), `expiry` (earliest first), `fifo` | pharma and food rotation; **Busy does not claim it** [W BUSY-BAT] |
| `schedule` | none, `H`, `H1`, `X`, `NDPS` | prescription register and prompt [W DR 65(3), 65(9)] |
| `serial` | `{ kind: imei or serial or tag; second: boolean; warrantyMonths; warrantyFrom: invoice or activation; numbering: manual or pattern }` | IMEI2, warranty start rule [Ws DOA], Busy's automatic or manual serials [W BUSY-SER] |
| `packs` | list of `{ code, qty, label }` | case and box codes [W GS1IN] |
| `weighed` | `{ plu, tare }` | grocery and agri [Ws GS1-RCN] |
| variants | **CF24 tracking fields (up to three), unchanged** | garments [C .wt-cf AD.3.2] |

### 6.2 Batch attributes

| Attribute | Notes |
|---|---|
| `no` | up to 20 characters so it fits `BchDtls.Nm`; the e-invoice needs at least 3, so the screen says when a batch will be left out of the IRN [C einvoice.ts:358]; the year limit on `ExpDt` is the same kind of warning (section 3.2) |
| `mfgDate`, `expiryDate` | both asked; `MM/YY` accepted and stored as the **last day of the month**, which Rule 96 and the 2025 cosmetics amendment both state [W DR 96; W COSM]; the e-invoice carries the date only to 2029 |
| `mrp` | pack MRP including taxes |
| `mrpRevision` | optional, dated: a lower price that caps the sale price on stock whose label still shows an older MRP (a price cut without re-stickering; the sale price is the lower of the printed MRP and the revision) [R pos-pharma-market section 3.3] |
| `rate` and **price levels at batch grain** | PTR and PTS are the **existing price levels** applied per batch (the shape of `StockItem.prices`, `types.ts:522`), so no new "rate 2, rate 3" fields [J] |
| `purchaseRate` | information and batch margin; valuation stays the item's method |
| `status` | `active`, `hold` (withdrawal pending, "Not for sale" [W DR 65(17)]), `expired`, `blocked` (recall) |
| `barcode` | the maker's GS1 code or the shop's own; a registry row (6.5) |
| `expiryWording` | optional: `expiry` (default) or `useBefore`; on a month-only date "use before" ends a month earlier [W COSM] |
| `attrs` | up to five name and value pairs for what a trade needs and the core does not: tile shade and caliber [Ws TILE], commodity grade and moisture, the maker where an item carries a generic name; the shape of Busy's serial descriptions [W BUSY-SER] |
| derived, not stored | received-on and supplier (from the purchase voucher), per-godown quantity (the engine already keeps it) |

**Identity [J, to confirm]:** item + `no` + `mrp`, because one batch can lawfully sit at two MRPs after a revision [W LM 18(3)] and Busy warns on duplicate batches [W BUSY-BAT]. The engine keys `byBatch` by name only today [C inventory.ts:141], so this is a real engine change, and the engine also keeps `byBatch` per item rather than per godown, so a batch position by godown is a second change that the sibling audit ranks first [R batch-serial-tracking section 9.1 row 1]; this model assumes both. **Against the pharmacy sibling [R pos-pharma-market R1]:** it asks for uniqueness per (item, batch number) with a dated MRP revision. The two are reconciled by keeping the **printed** MRP in the identity (re-stickered units and old-label units stay distinguishable, and a sale never exceeds what the pack says) and letting `mrpRevision` cap the sale price (so a price cut still binds); decision 4 stays open for the owner. **Storage [J]:** keep batch attributes on the line, as today, and **derive the batch index from the voucher log** (the codebase's own rule: engines are pure functions over the voucher log); a small record is needed only for `status`, which is an event, not a voucher fact (decision 6).

### 6.3 Serial attributes

| Attribute | Notes |
|---|---|
| `id` | the primary identifier (IMEI, appliance serial, tag number); stored up to 32 characters (Tally's alias limit [W TAL-BAR]); sent as `PrdSlNo` only when 20 or fewer; a start-plus-count range tool must step the 14-digit body and recompute the check digit, because consecutive units are not consecutive numbers [R batch-serial-tracking section 3.1.1] |
| `id2` | the second IMEI [Ws GSMA]; sent as an attribute or kept off the IRN |
| `kind` | `imei` (Luhn warn), `serial`, `tag` |
| state | in stock, sold, returned, purchase-returned, transferred (**derivable from vouchers**); DOA, in service, on approval, with karigar, at hallmark, written off (**need an event**, decision 6) |
| `purchase` | purchase voucher and **unit cost** |
| `sale` | sale voucher, price, customer |
| `mrp` | optional (Busy's per-serial MRP [W BUSY-SER]) |
| warranty | months, start rule, **end date derived**; `WrDt` on the IRN |
| attributes | up to ten name and value pairs (Busy allows ten descriptions [W BUSY-SER]); `AttribDtls` can carry them |
| `lot` | optional text up to 20 characters: the maker's lot, batch or date code; an attribute for recall and warranty, **not a stock dimension** |
| tag weights | for `tag`: gross, stone, net, purity, making or wastage, **HUID** |
| derived index | like `dashIndex`: one pass over the voucher log, never a stored table on a hot path [J] |

A serial's **lot** is its purchase voucher: a separate batch **dimension** on a serial item is not needed (Tally's advice to prefix a lot to the IMEI exists only because it has nowhere else to put it [Ws TAL-IMEI]). **But the maker's lot or date code is a recall and warranty fact for appliances, batteries, tyres and drugs with serialised packs** (Zoho's own serial report carries batch columns [R batch-serial-tracking section 3.1.6]), so a serial carries it as an **attribute** (`lot`), not as a second stock position. This changes the first draft. **A serial-tracked line has quantity one per serial**; the voucher line holds a list of serials.

### 6.4 Variant attributes (CF24-compatible)
- The variant is CF24's derived key (option ids joined by a control character, never stored on the line [C .wt-cf AD.3.2]). **Nothing about stock is added here.**
- Added by this research, per (item, variant key): an optional **barcode** (a registry row), an optional **MRP and rate override**. The addendum lists per-variant reorder levels, price lists, barcodes and SKUs as a different feature [C .wt-cf AD.5 row 25, AD.6], so **both additions are a slice of their own, not part of CF-9**.
- **Open interface:** CF24 defines positions per (item, variant) and (godown, variant) and keeps batch separate; a combination of variant and batch is **not defined** there. Garments rarely need both and pharma never needs variants, so v1 should **refuse the combination in the editor** rather than guess [J].
- **A serial-tracked item's colour or storage is an attribute of the unit**, not a CF24 tracking field: the unit already says what it is.
- **Counting by scan.** The addendum's count group (AD.3.3) says a Physical Stock count is always a count of the whole item at its godown, and a count made by variant is one group of `countPart` lines; a scan-based stock take of a variant item should produce exactly that voucher, one group per item and godown, not a second mechanism.

### 6.5 The barcode registry

One table, one resolver, replacing the single `alias`/`partNo` string for scanning purposes while leaving those fields working.

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `code` | the exact scanned text, 48 characters at most |
| `kind` | `item`, `variant`, `batch`, `serial`, `pack`, `scale`, `alias` |
| target | `itemId`, plus `variantKey`, `batchNo` and `mrp`, `serialId`, or `packQty` as the kind needs |
| `source` | `maker` (GS1) or `shop` (printed by the retailer) |
| `symbology` | a hint: `ean13`, `code128`, `qr`, `datamatrix`, `gs1-128` |
| `profile` | for `scale`: `{ prefix (digits or letters), itemDigits, valueDigits, value: weight, price or count, unit and decimals }`; one scale can print two profiles (weighed and counted) |
| `active` | true by default; a retired code stays in the registry and says so when scanned (a barcode reused across seasons, a superseded part number) |

**Resolution order [J]:** (1) exact registry hit; (2) GS1 parse if the text begins with a GS1 prefix (AI 01, 10, 17, 21 [Ws GS1-AI]); a drug pack's QR carries the CDSCO content in a layout each maker chooses [W CDSCO-FAQ], so parse tolerantly and say what was not recognised; (3) scale profile if the first digits match a configured prefix; (4) a 15-digit string that passes Luhn is an IMEI candidate; (5) the item's `alias` or `partNo` (today's behaviour); (6) unknown: say so, never guess. A variant's registry row stores the **option ids**, so renaming "Red" never breaks a printed tag [C .wt-cf CF4]. `Barcde` on the e-invoice is the item's primary code when it is 3 to 30 characters [W NIC].

### 6.8 Rules the model must obey [C CLAUDE.md, W back-nav-spec]
- **Accept more, never less; defaults frozen.** New batch prompt fields come after expiry in a walk that is asserted with them removed; a master field goes last or in an Alt+key grid (Phase 24 gotcha).
- **`optional` and memorandum vouchers never move stock or serial state** (`postsToLedgers`, the `!v.optional` guards).
- **A held or recalled value must be visible**: a batch or serial carried from the last line is shown, never inherited silently (Phase 21 gotcha).
- **Snapshot on the line**: MRP, batch attributes and HSN are copied onto the line when posted, or editing an item's price re-prices history (the Phase 23 GST-snapshot lesson).
- **Money via `fmtAmt`, quantities via `fmtQty`**; **never let magnitude shorthand near a quantity** (`parseQty` shares the arithmetic, not the magnitudes): an IMEI or a batch number is text, never parsed by `parseAmt`.
- **Sync:** a serial or batch is part of a line's identity in the Mismatch Report (`syncDiff.ts ROW_KEYS`); an older client must not erase it (the master-resave lesson); backup and Split Company carry it (`openingAlloc.batches` today drops the manufacturing date).
- **New dialogs** (serial entry, label designer) are `Box` with `onClose` and register a dirty probe [W back-nav-spec B5, B12]; a scan field must not leak keys to the screen beneath (Phase 25 gotcha).
- **No new batch on a sale (new).** A typed batch name on a sale creates a phantom batch today; a sale picks from stock, and only a return or a purchase may create a batch, with a reason on the return [R pos-pharma-market R3].
- **E-invoice honesty (new).** Send `ExpDt` and `WrDt` only inside the pattern the schema prints (years 2010 to 2029); say what was left out on the capture screen and in the preview; never invent a field or a number a government system issues (the Phase 29 rule).
- **MRP and RSP are tax facts for some goods (new).** For pan masala and tobacco the batch MRP is the tax base (section 3); it is snapshotted on the line like HSN and rate, and the rule that the highest printed price applies belongs to the valuation function, not to the typist.
- **Performance:** a distributor's serial index at 180,000 units a year is the scale to price [J]; derive in one pass, memoise, never recompute per keystroke (Phase 28 gotcha).

### 8.2 Validation plan (what to do before building)

5. **Confirm with a CA** the exchange-offer GST treatment and the margin-scheme record for a used phone taken as an exchange.

### 8.3 Risks
- **Scope:** serial, batch, variant, registry and labels are five features; the registry is the one that makes the others scan. Build order should start from the registry and the batch prompt, not from the label designer.
- **The engine decision (specific-unit cost) and batch identity (MRP in the key)** touch `stockPositions`, `byBatch`, Split Company, sync identity and the Mismatch Report; each needs the identity-by-reference proof the Phase 27 and CF24 suites use.
- **Regulatory drift:** the statutory ground moved repeatedly in the last year: QR scope on drugs (2022, extended in June 2026) [Ws DRUGS-QR], HUID extended to silver in 2025 [Ws HUID], apparel and footwear rates by unit value from 22 Sep 2025 [W GST56], retail-sale-price valuation for tobacco from 1 Feb 2026 [W RSP-NOTIF], and the e-invoice thresholds. Every statutory field should carry a dated source note, as the repo does for GST [C CLAUDE.md Phase 24], and the two tax rules (decisions 11 and 12) need a CA's confirmation before any default is shipped.
- **Vendor-claim bias:** most competitor evidence is marketing; the Busy floor is what Busy says, not what it does.
